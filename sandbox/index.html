Anneal learns from pictures with a single rule:

Keep whatever makes the whole description cheapest.

Everything else on this page follows from that. There are no weights to train and no numbers tuned by hand. Each time the engine considers a change, it works out the cost of describing everything it has seen, with and without the change, and keeps the change only if the cost falls.

The total cost of describing everything: structure (what the engine has stored) plus flow (the cost of explaining the recent pictures with what it has stored).

Whatever can't be explained cheaply: pixels nothing known accounts for. Learning is cooling.

Every cost is in one currency, taken from the world:

  • a pixel nobody explains (or one drawn wrongly) costs 1;
  • a pixel stored in memory costs 1;
  • a placement, "this part, here", costs 1 plus the cost of naming which part. That's a little more than 1, and grows slowly as the engine knows more parts.

To see a picture, the engine places the parts it knows wherever they fit, trying every possible starting placement, and keeps the cheapest whole explanation. A part can be placed anywhere, and more than once. Recognising something just means "placing it is cheaper than paying for its pixels".

Awake, a picture the engine can't explain cheaply is remembered whole. Then, at rest, pictures stop and it reorganises what it holds. It compares stored things with each other at every shift: what two things share becomes a part of its own, and recurring arrangements of parts become bigger things. Each move is tried, the bill is measured, and the move is kept only if the bill fell.

This is learning by comparison, not by averaging. A cross compared with a bar gives the bar exactly, at once, not a blur built up over thousands of examples.
