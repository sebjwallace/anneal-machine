Park walks as state transitions, with a held context

Each landmark is a column with a "now" cell and a "was" cell; a context is a column whose cell stays held. Remembered transitions fit when all their cells are lit, and the most detailed fit predicts the next state. Holding a context decides which way to go at the fountain. Drawn by hand to show the idea (change memory, src/engine/change.js); not a run of the engine.

Remembered transitions (before → after). Outlined: fits the current state. Green: the most detailed fit, which wins.

What it shows

How the machine remembers what happens next, and how a held context steers it. Each landmark is a column with two cells: "now" (where you are) and "was" (where you just were). When you move, the old now cell becomes the was cell. A context, such as wanting to feed the ducks, is a column of its own whose cell stays lit for the whole walk. A remembered transition is the set of cells that were lit together (now, was and the context) linked to the now cell that came next: one assembly across the columns.

Why it works this way

To predict, every remembered transition whose cells are all lit fits the current state, and the most detailed fit wins: the one that accounts for the most of what is lit. At the fountain, three ways out are known, but each was learnt with a context held. Holding "ducks" makes only the transition to the pond fit, so the walk rolls on by itself through the pond to the bench. Nothing chooses the path; the held cell makes one memory fit better than the others. The "was" cell gives direction: arriving at the fountain from the gate is a different state from arriving from the pond.

With no context, the first step still works, because at rest the machine compared the three walks and kept "gate to fountain" as a shared change without a context. At the fountain nothing fits, so it doesn't guess: it waits.

What's simplified here

In the engine, cells also carry positions (where each thing is), and shared changes are found by comparing remembered transitions at every shift. When two transitions fit with equal detail, today's engine takes the one seen most (a declared choice, decision 005); that case doesn't come up in this park.

Open questions

A context here is just held. Is it a want, with heat until something explains it? (That is the craving visual and experiment C59.) Should equal detail be refused, as completion refuses ties, rather than broken by "seen most"?
