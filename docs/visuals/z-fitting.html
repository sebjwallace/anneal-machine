Fitting by competition: the Z on a 4 × 4 grid

The owner's example: three assemblies that share D and M explain a Z; the other candidates drop out as misfits. Builds on the visual language.

How to walk through it

Press Next and watch the grid and the bills table together. Step 1: the Z in red, ten lit cells, all heat. Step 2: eight dashed paths appear; look at the Opens column: three at 2.5, three at 1.5, and ABF and LOP below zero, with F and L ringed in dashed red (claimed, not lit). Step 3: teal and coral bands land on the top and bottom rows; ABCD and MNOP tied with DGJM but share no cells, so there was nothing to decide. Step 4: a purple band runs down the diagonal; DGJM's Now has dropped to 0.5, but it still saves something, so it wins, and D and M sit under two bands. Step 5: the rest go faint as "dropped": the misfits. Step 6: the bill appears, 4.5 against 5.0 without DGJM, with no heat left. Step 7: an outline wraps the whole Z, the step at rest.

What it shows

Fitting by competition, in the owner's own example: a 4 × 4 grid of cells A to P, three assemblies that formed from abstractions (ABCD, MNOP, DGJM), and other candidates (CDG, CGO, GJM, ABF, LOP). Using an assembly costs about 1.5, an uncovered cell 1, a cell an assembly claims that isn't lit 1. The assembly that saves most takes its cells; every rival recounts on what is left; an assembly is taken only while it still saves something. The three win: the fewest assemblies, nothing claimed that isn't lit, nothing left uncovered. The others are misfits: too little coverage for their price, or claiming cells that aren't lit.

Two refinements. Overlap isn't penalised, it's wasted: D and M covered twice cost nothing extra, but DGJM pays full price for two new cells. And connection isn't rewarded while fitting: sharing D and M earns nothing in the bill. It pays at rest, where a Z that keeps recurring is composed into one super-assembly, one use at 1.5 instead of three at 4.5. A tie (three at 2.5) only means "don't guess" when taking one would rule out the other; ABCD and MNOP share no cells, so both can be right.

What's simplified

Drawn by hand with the engine's prices (C75), not a run of the engine. The price of a use, 1.5, depends on how many assemblies are known. Bands stand for assemblies here because these aren't rectangles; elsewhere an assembly is an outline. The same example at the level of columns and segmented cells is in z-columns.
