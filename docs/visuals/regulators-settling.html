Regulators settling together (entrainment)

Six linked regulators, each with its own resting state. Disturb one or more, or hold a load on one, and watch the heat spread along the links and the whole system settle together. Drawn by hand with a small simulation; the walkthrough underneath tells you what to press and what to look at.

How to read it (a walkthrough)

Start by looking at it at rest. There are six regulators, A to F, joined by lines. Each one has a resting level, shown by the dashed line through its middle, and the dot is where it actually is. At rest every dot sits on its line, nothing is orange, and the heat line on the right is flat at zero.

Press "Disturb one". A gets pushed up off its level and turns orange. Watch the lines coming out of A: they glow orange too, because A and its neighbours now disagree. The neighbours start moving towards A, and that pulls A back down. For a moment the heat spreads out across the group, then everything drifts back onto its line together. The heat line shoots up and slides back down to zero. Nobody fixes A; the group settles it.

Press "Disturb two". A gets pushed up and D gets pushed down, and there's a direct link between them. They pull on each other, so this settles quickly. Disturbances that oppose each other partly cancel out.

Press "Hold a load on one". This is the craving. A load is held on A and not lifted. A can't get back to rest on its own, so watch the neighbours: they shift a little towards A and stay there, sharing the strain. The heat line doesn't go back to zero; it levels off higher. The heat is spread thinly across the group instead of piling up in A. Now press "Calm" to lift the load, which is like the coffee arriving. Everything falls back to rest, and the heat line drops to zero.

Now press "Rhythms" at the top. This is entrainment proper. Each regulator now pulses: its circle grows and shrinks, and the dot goes round like a clock hand. At rest all six pulse in step. Press "Disturb one" and A gets knocked out of step; watch it get pulled back into the shared rhythm by its neighbours, like pendulum clocks on one wall. Then press "Hold a different pace on one": A wants to go faster. The others won't let it run away, and it can't fully slow down, so they settle into one shared rhythm with A slightly ahead. That's settling around a disturbance rather than removing it (allostasis).

What it shows

The principle that works inside a region (settling into a low-energy state) taken up a level: regulators that each have a resting state, linked so that each link wants its two ends to agree. A disturbance in one is heat that spreads along the links; the others respond, and the whole system moves downhill in energy until nothing more can fall.

The held load shows something the owner pointed out: while a disturbance can't be resolved, the neighbours take on part of the stress, so it is spread thinly instead of concentrated in one part. That keeps the system going long enough for the disturbance to be resolved, as load spreads through a sound structure. It is still heat, but flowing and shared rather than trapped in one place. What this is about, in the end, is balance.

What's simplified here

This is drawn by hand with a small simulation, not the engine. Levels: each regulator is pulled back to its rest, and each link pulls its two ends together; the strengths are chosen to make it easy to see, not measured. Rhythms: each regulator has the same pace and each link pulls its two ends into step (the usual way coupled rhythms are modelled). The "heat" is the energy of that simulation: how far each is from rest, plus how much each link's two ends disagree.
