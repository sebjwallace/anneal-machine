# Visuals

Animations, interactive pieces and illustrations, used to agree how an idea works. Each one is a single HTML file
that opens in any browser with nothing else needed, and explains itself: under the visual is its story (what it shows,
why it works that way, what's simplified, open questions, and where it comes from). They are drawn by hand to show an
idea, not runs of the engine, unless they say otherwise.

| Visual | Shows | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| [assemblies.html](assemblies.html) | **The visual language** the others build on: a cell as a dot, an assembly as an outline around its active cells, a super-assembly as an outline around outlines; composition (roof and wall arranged into a house) and decomposition (a house and a shed compared, their shared wall pulled out as an abstraction). With a walkthrough and Sagan's four words. | Glossary: assembly, composition, decomposition, abstraction |
| [columns.html](columns.html) | Columns as dimensionality expansion: nine input cells, a column of cells over each, an input pattern lighting one cell per column, those cells linked into an assembly; a second pattern sharing inputs but not cells; a cue completing only one; and the blend you get without columns (as in a classic Hopfield network). With a walkthrough. | Glossary: column, cell, assembly; CTR-1 CAP-2, CAP-3 |
| [segments.html](segments.html) | Columns of segmented cells: each cell cut into slices (its segments); two assemblies share two cells (their abstraction) with each memory's links on its own slice; scattered activity wakes the shared cells falsely when links are pooled, not with segments; a real cue completes one assembly. Each slice is an AND of its links, the cell an OR of its slices. With a walkthrough. | C35 (dendritic segments); CTR-1 CON-9, CAP-3 |
| [segments-zoom.html](segments-zoom.html) | One cell belonging to three assemblies, zoomed in: its nine links in one pool wake it falsely on scattered activity; sorted onto one segment per memory, they don't, and the segment that fires says which memory woke it. With a walkthrough. | C35; CTR-1 CON-9 |
| [z-fitting.html](z-fitting.html) | Fitting by competition in the owner's Z example on a 4 × 4 grid: three assemblies sharing D and M explain the Z; the other candidates drop out as misfits (too little coverage for their price, or claiming cells that aren't lit); overlap is wasted, not penalised; connection pays only at rest, where the Z is composed. With a walkthrough. | C75; the owner's example |
| [z-binding.html](z-binding.html) | Composition by binding a sample: three parts of a Z on their own cells; the Z lights whole columns and segments pick the cell; two sampled cells per part grow segments over each other, and later the top row alone brings back the whole Z; with one per part, nothing. With a walkthrough. | C76; the owner's idea |
| [decomposition-cut.html](decomposition-cut.html) | Decomposition done by cells: a shed partly wakes the stored house; the cells' states (fits / predicted, not there / unexplained) draw the cut; the wall becomes one shared assembly on the same cells, each whole is rebound by two cells per part, it costs fewer links, and recall from the house's roof brings back the house, not the shed. With a walkthrough. | C78 |
