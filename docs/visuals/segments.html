Segments: columns of segmented cells

Each cell cut into slices, its segments, so a cell shared by two memories keeps their evidence apart. Builds on columns. Drawn by hand to show the idea; not a run of the engine.

Cells

How to walk through it

Start with With segments selected and press Next. On step 1 every cell is a circle cut into three slices. On step 2, look at A's four cells: their teal slices are tinted, and A's links run between teal slices. On step 3, C arrives in purple. Look at the 5th and 7th columns: those two cells have both a teal slice and a purple slice. That's the shared part, the abstraction, with each memory's evidence on its own slice. On step 4, one A cell and one C cell light, and nothing else happens, because no slice reaches two. Now press One pool on this same step (the toggle keeps the step): the two shared cells get red rings, woken for nothing. On step 5, two of A's cells light. With segments, the teal slices in the shared cells fill solid and the cells get teal rings: A completes, and the purple slices stay pale. With one pool they wake too, but nothing says whether it was for A or for C.

What it shows

Columns separate memories across cells: different contexts get different cells. But one cell is still reused by every memory that has the same input in the same context, and that sharing is what makes overlap mean similarity, and the shared cells an abstraction. If a shared cell pools all its links, evidence from different memories adds up, and it wakes for memories that aren't there. Segments split a cell's links by memory. Each segment is an AND: it fires only when two or more of its own links are active. The cell is an OR of its segments: any one memory is enough to wake it. So evidence is counted per memory, never summed across memories (CTR-1 CON-9), and which segment fired tells you which memory woke the cell.

A segment is not a cell. Nothing links to it and it never shows downstream: it only gathers evidence for its cell. That is why segments are slices of a cell rather than more cells. They add ways of being woken without changing what the cell means, so the shared code stays shared.

What's simplified

Three slices per cell, two in use; real cells could have many. Links are drawn between slices, but each link starts from a whole active cell and lands on a slice of another. In the engine today, C35 showed the pooling failure: about 80% wrong recalls with five things stored, fixed by counting evidence per memory. The owner's earlier session on the logic of cells (OR, AND, then NOT and NAND) goes further than this and is not covered here.

Where it comes from

The owner's dendritic-segments idea (C35); CTR-1 CON-9 (cells carry no numbers; evidence counted per memory) and CAP-3 (auto-associative memory). Builds on columns; the zoomed-in view of one cell is in segments-zoom.
