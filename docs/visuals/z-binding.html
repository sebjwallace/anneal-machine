Composition by binding a sample: the Z

Three parts, each on its own cells, tied together by two sampled cells from each, so that any one part brings back the whole Z. Builds on segments and the Z. Drawn by hand to show the idea; tested in C76.

How to walk through it

Start with Two cells per part and press Next. Hover over a column to see its letter, or press Show letters. Step 1: three assemblies on their own cells, teal along the top row, pink in G and J, coral along the bottom; no column holds two. Step 2: the Z lights ten whole columns, and every cell in them turns faint red: all candidates. Step 3: one cell per column wins, the one whose segment fits. Step 4: purple lines join six cells (A, C, G, J, N, P), each with a purple half, the new binding segment; the panel shows 30 links against 90 for wiring everything. Step 5: only the top row is seen. Step 6: rings appear on G, J, N and P, woken through the purple links. Step 7: rings on M and O, completed by the bottom row's own segments, and "Z complete: yes". Then press One cell per part and step to the end: only B is sampled from the row, the binding never sees two, and nothing comes back.

What it shows

Composition by binding a sample, the owner's idea. Parts that fit together are tied not by wiring every cell to every other, but by a few of their active cells each growing one new segment over the others. When one part later comes back, its sampled cells wake the binding, the binding wakes the other parts' sampled cells, and each part completes itself. A cell is an OR of its segments; each segment is an AND of its links. Because a segment fires at two active links (the bar from the prices), the sample needs two cells from each part, and no more.

Each assembly has its own cells: assemblies share a cell only when they genuinely share a part (the same input in the same context). With the diagonal as G and J, no column holds two cells: the parts fit together by being active together, not by sharing. Bound once, from one moment, the binding is an experience; bound because the Z keeps recurring, it is a composition paying its way: one mechanism, two keeping rules.

What's simplified

Drawn by hand. Which cell wins in each column (step 3) is shown, not computed. The binding is made all at once; when it should be kept is not shown. Tested in C76: two cells per part always brought the whole composition back, with under a quarter of the links of wiring everything, far fewer intrusions and about a fiftieth of the work; one per part never did.
