Decomposition by the cut: the house and the shed

A shed partly wakes the stored house it resembles; the cells' own states draw the cut, and the shared wall becomes one assembly on the same cells. Builds on binding a sample. Drawn by hand; tested in C78.

How to walk through it

Press Next and keep an eye on the panel on the right. Step 1: the house, stored whole, one grey outline around it. Step 2: the shed lights 15 columns, and every cell in them is faint red, because nothing yet predicts which cell. Step 3: three colours: teal across the wall (it fits), dashed red rings on the house's roof (predicted, not there), solid red on the shed's roof (unexplained). That is the comparison, and nothing searched for it. Step 4: a dashed line marks the cut, exactly between those states. Step 5: three assemblies: a teal outline round the wall, on the same cells as before, and a coral band for the house's roof and a pink one for the shed's roof, on fresh cells. Step 6: purple links for the house's binding, blue for the shed's, two cells from each part. Step 7: the panel shows 168 links against 420, so the cut pays. Step 8: recall from the house's roof: the wall gets teal rings as it comes back, and the shed's roof stays dashed. The panel shows why: all four of the house binding's cells are active, against two of the shed's.

What it shows

Decomposition done by cells, Sagan's "analyzes". A shed arrives that shares a wall with a stored house. Its columns light all their cells, so the house's wall cells see each other active and the house partly wakes: the comparison ("intercompares") happens by itself, with no list of parts compared pair by pair. Every cell's state then draws the cut: lit and predicted (the wall fits), predicted but not lit (the house's roof), lit but predicted by nothing (the shed's roof). Cutting there, the fitting cells become their own assembly on the same cells, the abstraction, really shared; the house's roof becomes its own; the shed's roof gets fresh cells; each whole is rebound by two sampled cells per part. It is kept only if it pays, counted in links.

A shared part needs competition between whole memories: the wall belongs to both bindings, so on its own it would wake both. From the house's roof, the house's binding has more of itself active than the shed's, so it wins.

What's simplified

Drawn by hand, with link counts for these shapes (a wall of 12 cells, roofs of 3). Tested in C78 on random shapes: the cut was right in every family; the pair cost 114 links against 264 for both wholes (parts of 6 cells there); with the bindings competing, each roof brought back its own whole (96-97% clean at 64 cells per column, 200 families). The cut needs the top level of support (C74) to keep chance predictions from other memories out.
