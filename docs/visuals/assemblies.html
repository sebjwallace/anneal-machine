The visual language: assembly, composition, decomposition

How we draw cells, assemblies and assemblies of assemblies, so every later visual can build on the same look. Drawn by hand to show the ideas; not a run of the engine.

How to walk through it

Assembly. Press Assembly, then Next twice. First, every grey dot is a quiet cell. Then some of them light up together. Last, an outline appears around the lit cells. That outline is how an assembly is drawn from now on: the same set of cells means the same thing wherever it shows up.

Composition. Press Composition. Step 1 shows two separate assemblies, a roof and a wall, each with its own outline. Press Next and watch them slide into an arrangement, the roof above the wall. Press it once more and a purple outline wraps around both. That's the super-assembly, an outline around outlines. The inner outlines stay, because the parts are still assemblies in their own right.

Decomposition. Press Decomposition. Step 1 shows a house and a shed, each stored whole: one outline and one colour each. On step 2, look for the amber cells. Comparing the two finds what they share: the same cells in the same arrangement. On step 3 the shared piece appears in the middle as an assembly of its own, the wall, which is an abstraction. Each original now has two outlines inside it: the wall, plus what was left over.

What it shows

The brain does much more than just recollect. It intercompares, it synthesizes, it analyzes, it generates abstractions. Carl Sagan, Cosmos (1980)

The owner's four words map onto the engine. Intercomparing is the core act: two assemblies side by side give what they share and what differs. Synthesis is composition: assemblies arranged into a larger one. Analysis is decomposition: an assembly taken apart into sub-assemblies. Abstraction is the shared part that comparing finds. The wall is in both the house and the shed, and particular to neither. Decomposition and abstraction come out of the same comparison: the shared piece is the abstraction, and splitting each original into that piece plus the rest is the decomposition.

The shared wall is the lowest rung of abstraction: literally the same cells. Higher rungs are frames, which share constants ("animal eats animal"), and types, which share surroundings rather than cells (sun, sea and fire in C66).

What's simplified

The outlines are a drawing convention. In the engine, an assembly is a set of cells across columns, and nothing draws a boundary around it. Composition and decomposition at rest are kept only if they lower energy. Here the house and shed are taken apart because describing both through one wall is cheaper than storing each whole; the visual doesn't show those prices. Awake, a composition is temporary: it explains an input for now and is not stored. That is fitting, which comes next in this language.

Where it comes from

The glossary terms assembly, super-assembly, composition, decomposition, comparing and abstraction. In the code, decomposition is the rest move that pulls two parts apart where they overlap; lasting composition is the compose move (both in src/engine/see.js). Comparing is CTR-1 CAP-1.
