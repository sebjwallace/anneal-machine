# Philosophy

*Draft by the assistant, from conversations with the owner, for the owner to correct. Held as a premise to explore, not a conclusion.*

## Intelligence as settling
The starting idea is that intelligence is a self-organising web of fuzzy state machines, and that what drives it is energy:
- Something that doesn't fit (a conflict, an unexplained picture) is **heat**.
- Heat makes the machine changeable: structure loosens and reconfigures.
- As things fit together again, it **cools**, and what cooled into shape becomes memory.

The machine is, in that sense, an elaborate homeostasis machine. That earlier name was "homeostatic state machines", plural. Anneal is the latest form of it: annealing is heating metal and letting it cool slowly, so it settles into a calmer, stronger structure.

## Build only what pays
Structure isn't free. Every part, layer or memory the machine keeps has to earn its place by making the whole description cheaper. Nothing exists "just in case". If one layer can do it, there is one layer. Perfect memory isn't the goal either: good-enough memory is, and exact records belong outside the machine, in something like a notebook it can read and write.

## Compare, don't average
The machine learns the way people often do, by setting two things side by side and asking what they share and how they differ. What they share becomes an idea of its own; what differs is what's left. That is quicker and cleaner than slowly averaging over many examples, and it works on things (shapes) and on happenings (changes) alike.

## No crutches
The machine has to learn for itself. Fixed architecture is fine: columns, cells, the rule of energy. Task-specific rules, filters and numbers tuned to make a test pass are not, because they would make the results an illusion. Where something is still a choice, it's written down openly.

## The other half of the cycle: a gap is heat, and heat lowers the bar
*From the owner, November 2026 (experiments C44–C51).*

Everything before this was the first half: the machine settles what it is shown. The second half is what it does with
something that **doesn't** settle: a question with no answer, a goal with no way to it, two things that should connect
and don't.

- **A gap is heat.** A question is high energy because it is a gap: things that don't connect, energy that can't flow.
  That heat doesn't stay outside. It comes into the machine.
- **Heat lowers the bar.** Normally an assembly wakes only when two of its parts are already active (two clues in one
  memory). Heat lowers that bar, so a single active part is enough to wake the assemblies it belongs to. Many
  assemblies can be active at once (sparse, spread over cells), so more of them come in to fill the gap: things are
  thrown up into the air.
- **The ends are held.** The question's two ends (where you are, where you want to be) stay active the whole time, like
  the two poles of a lightning strike: the charge holds until a path closes.
- **The gap closes, the bar rises.** Where activity from both ends meets, the connection is made. The bar goes back up,
  only what is supported from both sides stays, and the route settles. That is cooling: energy has flowed from the
  question to the answer.
- **Bigger memories are shortcuts.** Activity rises through bigger assemblies and comes back down through them, like
  taking a plane instead of walking the streets.
- **No way through is an answer too.** If nothing meets and nothing new wakes, the machine stays hot: it knows it can't
  see a way.
- **Imagination is checked against experience.** Settled answers can be wrong: general memory can join things that were
  never really connected. Remembered experiences, kept by their own rule (keep what happened, fade what's never seen
  again), are the cheap check: a plan whose every step was lived somewhere is trusted; a step nobody ever took is a
  misfit, heat again. Testing in the world is the expensive check, for what memory can't settle.

So the machine is a pump: it takes heat from outside (a gap, a misfit) and moves it into structure and into thinking,
until things connect and settle. The pump itself costs something (the work of thinking is real), but the heat it
resolves would otherwise stay trapped.

Two corollaries the owner drew:
- **A crutch is trapped heat.** A patch in the structure hides a misfit instead of resolving it: high energy held in
  structure. The real low-energy answer usually removes structure.
- **Forgetting is cooling too.** General memory lowers energy by removing structure; remembered experiences keep a lot
  of it. Keeping what matters and forgetting what doesn't may be part of the same settling.

Evidence: C44 (pushing from both ends joins never-walked routes), C46–C47 (checking against remembered experiences
catches every made-up route; they need their own rule), C51 (the moving bar gives exactly C44's results: pushing is the
ordinary recall rule with the bar lowered while a gap is open). Not yet in the engine.

## Inspired, not copied
The work draws on associative memory, Hebbian learning, Numenta's columns and assemblies, and sparse coding, as inspiration rather than a template. It isn't trying to copy the brain or deep learning. It learns online, from nothing, on an ordinary CPU, and aims to stay far cheaper to run than today's AI.

## The deeper why
Much of the world, physically and mentally, is running hot, and the usual ways of cooling down tend to repeat the same destructive cycles. A machine that turns heat into useful structure, rather than letting it rise and fall, is worth exploring as a question, not offered as an answer.
