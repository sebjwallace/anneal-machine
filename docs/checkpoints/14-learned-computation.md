## In one paragraph

Associative memory can compute, and can learn to. A focus that reads, writes and moves over a space, steered only by
completion, competition, binding and heat, carried out column addition, subtraction, multiplication by one digit and
the whole of long multiplication from one cue, with no controller (C149, C170). Then, step by step, what had been
written by hand was learned instead: first the per-column step from worked examples (C142, C150), then the order of
ways of working (C171), then a whole way of working from a page showing only the question, with the worked answer as a
target to agree with (C172). After that the work turned to *how* the memory learns: one memory keeping what settles and
choosing by competition (C173), each lesson built from the last, brought up by association and adapted where the heat
is (C175 to C178), the adapting made heat's own (C179 to C185), and finally abstraction from overlap: hubs of shared
relations among the ways of working it has kept, used for completion, learned the hardest way of working (the second
row) in every seed (C186). The stress test (C187) found the limit: those relations are held too exactly to reach a
lesson laid out differently.

## Why it matters

Until C137 the engine recognised and completed but did nothing: no procedure, no state carried through steps. The
owner's goal is learned computation on associative memory, a machine that learns to do things from experience in the
same currency it uses to perceive, without a controller, a training phase or code doing its thinking (CTR-1 GOL-1,
GOL-2). This arc shows the dynamics are enough to *run* a non-trivial algorithm, and that most of what's needed to
*learn* one can come from the memory: keeping, choosing, transfer from earlier lessons, context, and abstraction from
overlap. It also changed how we think about learning. The driving analogy carries it: a learner driver doesn't learn
to drive from scratch on a motorway; they learn the controls, then the car park, then quiet roads, and each new skill
is an old one brought up by the situation and adjusted where it hurts. What carries over is not a recording of
earlier drives but what many drives had in common.

## The journey (how one idea led to the next)

**Frames and places (C137–C141).** A filler carried through a frame with no binding to it, once transitions are learned
only on what changed (C137, C138). Attention among registers learned by heat (C139). Variables as places relative to a
frame, not values (C140). Each step of a walk as a gate keyed on where the walker was and the move (C141). These are the
pieces every later experiment uses.

**An algorithm learned, then judged by the engine (C142–C148).** Column addition's per-column step learned from six
worked sums, 4,000 of 4,000 right on longer sums at new positions (C142), with a search steered by heat (C143) and
facts recalled by completion (C144). Completion couldn't judge a written digit, so rivals from place were added: two
members compete if they've never been together but share company (C145). Lanes let several hypotheses be judged at
once (C146); heat lowering the bar around a hot cell chose among them (C147); and the jam where a wrong step, once bound,
could never be outgrown was fixed by "copies count": the same structure formed again is the same cells again, and the
one that exists more times wins (C148).

**The engine does the sum (C149–C155).** The milestone: a sum done entirely by the engine's dynamics, the code only
writing the numbers in and reading the answer out (C149). The step learned by the circuit that runs it (C150), then
judging, choosing and binding moved into the circuit too: cooling as a cell, a misfit stopping a lane's clock,
"Settled" as a gate (C151). Two procedures as separate steps interfered (C152); one step that reads the sign like a
digit served addition, subtraction and times one digit (C153 to C155).

**Long multiplication breaks one step (C156–C164).** The first row multiplies by the wrong digit (C156). Holding a value
fixed it (C157, C158). Choosing which way of working a page needs then became the problem: binding each way of working
with what was in view, and keeping what its episodes share, took right recall from under a quarter to about 80% (C159
to C162). A second-row way of working by hand made whole two-row multiplications right for the first time (C163). The
memory's own offers settled most examples but not all (C164).

**Procedures inside the memory, by hand (C165–C170).** From [schema and spatial memory](../theories/schema-and-spatial-memory.md):
a procedure as structure that biases completion, handing over to the next way of working on an arrival (the work
running out, native to competition), jumping back to a start held as a latch, and letting go when done. The whole of
long multiplication, first row to final sum, from one cue, 200 of 200, quiet at the end (C170). All of it by hand.

**Learning by doing and checking (C171–C172).** A finished page doesn't show order, so the working readable on the page
taught nothing; held as a target the memory's own writing must agree with, exactly one composition settled (C171).
The second row's whole way of working was found from a page showing only the question (C172).

**Keeping and choosing in one memory (C173).** What settles is kept; recall is by competition with copies counting;
nothing is rebuilt between examples. Repetition removed coincidences. Equally right ways of working tied for ever; a
declared tie-break ("the first formed wins") broke it once and repetition kept it.

**Gradual learning and context (C174–C178).** The owner's picture: basics first, each skill built from the last, pieces
brought up by association and fitted where the heat is. Times one digit, from 1,112 tries from scratch to 12 by
adapting what adding brought up (C176). The second row from times one digit, 412 runs against 136,922 blind (C177).
Seeing what's at each place near the start, and keeping it with the way of working that did the work, let every kind
of page choose its own way of working, 200 of 200, and made the second row cheaper again (C178).

**Making the adapting heat's own (C179–C185).** Three candidate energy rules compared; heat lowering the bar, nearest
first and widening, was cheapest and steady (C179). As heat that builds up on what's to blame, one try at a time, it
was never fooled but stuck where two parts had to move together (C180). As energy on the substrate with four simple
rules, it churned (C181). A calibration exchange with the owner then set the design out in
[convergence and the map](../theories/convergence-and-the-map.md): a part holds by convergence, not strength; one
change at a time, judged worse, no change or better; no tracing back; every attempt forms structure (the map); residue
heat bound to its context, generalising through a sparse sample; no totals (inclusion instead: a new best must cool
everything the old did, and more); a basin fills with heat and pushes you out. Built: no churn (C182); residue on a
sparse sample, 2 of 3 seeds (C183); nothing undone drifted, 0 of 3 (C184); anchored and widening breadth-first, 1 of 3,
trapped by a coincidental step (C185). The two failures are the classic pair: objective search sticks in local
optima, and novelty search diverges.

**Abstraction from overlap (C186–C187).** The owner's point that spatial memory comes first and schema is distilled from
its overlap, and that abstraction is a web, not layers (each thing compared with everything else, in any direction).
Every pair of kept ways of working compared; what they share becomes a hub; hubs compared in turn. Hubs of shared
relations (how one part sits relative to another), used for completion so that related parts snap along together, learned the second row in 3 of 3 seeds; hubs of shared positions were no better than none (C186). The stress
test, the final adding of long multiplication (adding laid out differently, rows far apart), was not learned with hubs
or without (C187): relations held as exact offsets don't bleed into a lesson whose offsets differ.

## How it works (the ideas that held up)

- **The space and a focus.** Everything is written into a space as assemblies at places. A focus reads, writes and
  moves; moves are gates (C141). Variables are places relative to the focus or a start, never values (C140).
- **Facts by completion, judging by rivals from place.** Facts are assemblies completed from their inputs (C144). What
  competes is learned from what has never been together (C145).
- **A way of working** is a frame: what it holds, reads, writes, where carries go, which way it moves, where it starts.
  One way of working, reading the sign like a digit, serves several kinds of sum (C153).
- **Procedures** are structure that biases completion: hand-overs on arrivals, jumps to a start held as a latch,
  letting go by competition (C165 to C170).
- **Learning by doing and checking.** The worked answer is a target the memory's own writing must agree with; cooling
  is agreement; "Settled" is a gate over all the working (C151, C171).
- **Keeping and choosing.** What settles is bound, only the way of working that did the work, with the view at its
  start (C176, C178). Recall is by competition; copies count (C148, C173).
- **Transfer by association.** A new page's view brings up the nearest kept way of working, and it is adapted toward
  the heat (C176 to C178).
- **The map and residue heat.** Every attempt leaves structure, whatever its outcome. Residue heat marks what hurt, in
  its situation, through a sparse sample so it generalises loosely (C182, C183).
- **Inclusion, not totals.** A new best must cool everything the old did, plus more (C185).
- **Hubs of relations.** Overlap between kept ways of working gives hubs of how parts sit relative to one another; a
  move completes through them (C186).

## What it showed (the numbers worth remembering)

- Column addition learned from six examples: 4,000 of 4,000 on 4 to 8 digits at new positions (C142).
- Rivals from place: every right digit kept and every wrong one dropped, 9,000 of 9,000 (C145).
- The engine does the sum (C149); whole long multiplication from one cue, 200 of 200 (C170).
- One composition settles only when the working is a target (C171).
- Times one digit: 1,112 tries from scratch, 12 adapted from adding in one memory (C176).
- The second row: 136,922 blind, 412 from times one digit (C177), 258 with the view at the start (C178).
- Context: all four kinds of page choosing their own way of working, 200 of 200 (C178; 0 for second rows in C177).
- Heat's own adapting: 2 of 3 (C183), 0 of 3 (C184), 1 of 3 (C185); relation hubs 3 of 3 (C186).
- The stress test: final adding 0 of 6 (C187).

## What didn't work (don't redo these without a new idea)

- Two procedures as separate steps sharing parts: they interfere (C152).
- A frame's episodes stored only as full views: they tie over incidental digits (C159).
- Overlaps against every earlier view: they grow into mostly coincidences (C161).
- The working readable on the page as the teacher: order can't be read off a finished page (C171).
- Spreading from the gap: pieces don't come right in the order the work is done (C179).
- Simple local energy rules on the substrate: they churn (C181). What the code's version held without saying so: one
  change at a time, heat independent of how many neighbours a part has, parts that do their job holding.
- Residue bound to the whole situation: it bars the changes that are needed (C182).
- Undoing nothing: it drifts (C184).
- Hubs of positions: they protect nothing useful (C186).

## What was given by hand (how much to trust it)

Every result here rests on things the experimenter supplied, all listed in the [crutch register](../../experiments/crutch-register.md):

- **The encoder:** worked examples laid out in a grid, the working marked as a target, padding, and the view at the start.
- **The facts:** single-digit facts given, not learned.
- **The lesson order:** the school order, fixed in advance.
- **The first lesson:** found by search.
- **The tie-break:** "the first formed wins".
- **The rule reading the structure:** which part gives way and in what order alternatives are tried. This is still the code's.
- **Comparing:** pairs are compared at the start of adapting instead of continuously in the background.
- **Completion through hubs:** done by the most widely shared relation, a rule in the code.

The machinery that runs procedures, and the learning, are sketches, not the engine.

## What to show people

The driving story is the clearest: the engine first drove a route someone had written out for it, step by step (C170).
Then it learned the route from watching worked examples with the answer as something to agree with (C172). Then it
learned to drive gradually, each new road an old skill brought up by the situation and adjusted where it hurt (C176 to
C178). Last, it noticed what its drives had in common, how things sit relative to one another, and used that to learn
the hardest manoeuvre every time (C186). The honest limit is the same story: it knows the roads it has driven exactly,
and can't yet see that a differently laid-out junction is the same kind of junction (C187).

## Limits and open questions

- **Relations too exact.** Hubs hold exact offsets; a fuzzier relation (below, above, between, some distance along)
  may be what lets a lesson laid out differently be reached. How fuzziness arises in the substrate, perhaps from sparse
  samples as with residue heat, is open.
- **Comparing as a background process.** Hubs are formed when adapting starts; the owner's picture is continuous
  intercomparing in any direction, perhaps in a second volume (DEF-10). Deferred.
- **Pairs and local optima.** Changes that only help together; when to give up on a direction; how a basin fills with
  heat and pushes the search out, without totals and without undoing nothing.
- **Coincidences learned early** can carry an anchor into the wrong basin (C185).
- **Adapting and completion are still the code's rules**, reading the structure the substrate holds. Making them the
  substrate's own dynamics is the largest gap.
- **The ladder.** Everything so far uses worked examples with all the working shown; partial working and answers only
  are untouched.
- **Crutches standing:** the encoder help, facts given, the first lesson by search, the lesson order, the tie-break.
- **The language.** The owner's decision (DEC-14): continue in the AMP language, where learning is composed from
  primitives on cell sets (overlaps, subsets) within the constraints of associative memory, so that what the code was
  doing becomes either a primitive or visibly outside. JavaScript stays for the harness.
- **Words to agree:** those listed at the top.
- **Not yet in the ledger:** C92 to C105.
