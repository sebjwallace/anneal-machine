# Feelings as heat

A philosophical theory, from conversation (13 November 2026). It reads human feelings as heat and the flow of energy,
in the same terms the engine uses. It is a lens, not a claim that people are only this, and not every feeling is a
problem to be solved. Nothing here has been tested. The last section says what it could mean for the machine.

## 1. The claim

What we feel is how heat sits in us and whether it can flow. Heat here means parts that don't fit cheaply together:
a misfit, a gap, a void. Some heat is trapped inside, with nowhere to go, and we feel it as distress. Some heat sits at
the edge between what we hold and what is out there, with a path in front of it, and we feel it as curiosity and
excitement. When the parts inside fit and nothing is trapped, energy flows, and we feel settled and fulfilled.

## 2. Misfit, not expectation

The usual story says feelings come from expectations not being met. This theory prefers misfit, gap or void, because
a misfit doesn't need anyone to know what should have been there. Two parts can fail to settle together without
anyone, inside or out, being able to say what the right fit would be. Longing is the clear case: the gap is felt
before it is understood. This is also closer to the engine, where heat is just parts that don't fit, whether or not
anything predicted it.

## 3. Heat trapped inside

Some feelings are heat with no way out.

- **Longing**: a gap felt before it is understood.
- **Sorrow and grief**: a world where something or someone is gone, which still has to settle around the shape they
  left.
- **Ambivalence**: two things that can't both fit.
- **Stress**: more misfit than can be resolved at once.

Trapped heat goes round and round, and builds as pressure.

## 4. Patching

One response to trapped heat is to cover it rather than resolve it. Rationalisation is the human form: a plausible
story that holds a misfit in place. Psychology documents this well; people readily give reasons for what they do that
aren't the real reasons. A patch is a structure on top of a structure, and structure is held energy. A story that
covers a misfit tends to need more stories to hold it up. The heat is not gone. It is held, and it costs.

## 5. Heat on the outside

Excitement and curiosity are heat too, but of another kind. They are not trapped inside. They draw you outward. They
come from realising that what you know and have lived is not all there is: the frontier. Exploring, playing and
experimenting is following that pull, and learning is closing the gap between inside and outside. It is a positive
pull.

The difference between distress and excitement may not be the amount of heat, but whether it has a way to flow. In
physical terms, heat only does work when it has a gradient and a path down it. Without a path it is pressure.

Research on curiosity fits this: people are most drawn to gaps of a middling size. Too small and there is nothing to
learn (boredom). Too big and there is no way to close it (anxiety). The pull is strongest where the gap is big enough
to matter and small enough to close.

## 6. Happiness and fulfilment

Happiness is coherence: the parts inside fit, little heat is trapped, and energy flows. It is not zero heat. A system
with no heat would be still, and would learn nothing. The fulfilled person has nothing trapped, so they are free to feel
the pull from outside and follow it. Coherence inside is what lets curiosity happen. The reverse is familiar: under
stress, curiosity fades, because the energy goes into holding things together.

## 7. Set points

A thermostat settles towards a set point given to it. People have set points too, and the heat they feel is distance
from them. For a machine, the deepest set points must come from outside, from whoever builds it and for what purpose.
Everything else can settle and differentiate, but the anchors are given. That is where values enter the design.

## 8. Resolve, not mask

A system that reduces heat can always cheat: it can cover the measure of a misfit instead of resolving the misfit.
That is rationalisation in machine form. So the rule against patches is more than engineering taste. A machine that
truly resolves rather than masks is one that can be trusted.

## 9. The purpose

The machine's purpose, in this framing, is to take the heat in the systems it serves (including the Earth, which is
holding more heat than it sheds, and the human systems of energy, food, water and institutions that no longer fit the
world they sit in) and turn it into structure: knowledge of how things can fit together again, so energy can flow and
things can settle, and suffering lessens. For that, the heat it works on must come from those systems, measured
honestly, and success must be judged by whether those systems settle, not by whether the machine's own numbers do.

Nothing in the engine needs to suffer for this. For the machine, heat can be just a quantity: a gap with a size and a
place.

## 10. What it could mean for the engine

- **Two kinds of heat.** Heat inside a region (misfit among what it holds) and heat at its edge (what arrives that it
  can't yet explain). The first is resolved by rest and settling; the second by going out and learning.
- **Curiosity instead of puppeteering.** Today we choose what the engine sees: the walks, the views, the days. If the
  engine could feel the gap at its edge and go where that gap is closable, it would choose its own experience. The
  drive would come from heat, not a rule we wrote. This is a first step to an active learner.
- **Coherence before curiosity.** A region full of trapped heat should explore less. Settling comes first.
- **Closable gaps.** The pull should be strongest where the gap is neither trivial nor out of reach. How to tell that
  without a tuned number is an open question.

## Open questions

- Can the engine tell heat that has a path from heat that doesn't, without being told?
- What would a closable gap look like in its own terms, with nothing tuned?
- Where do the given set points sit, and how few can there be?
- How would we test that the machine resolves rather than masks?
