# The tilting landscape

A theory note, continuing from [units, flow and the states of a bond](units-flow-and-bond-states.md). **Nothing here has
been tested.** In one exchange, a run of new ideas moved the picture a long way:

- completion became the sink;
- unresolved basins became the source of a distributed pull, like gravity;
- the landscape became something that tilts with context;
- switches, or latches, turned out not to be needed at all.

What remains is very small: **pathways, and units rolling down a landscape shaped by where the units are.** The ideas are
recorded in the order they came, because each one changed the one before.

Related: [units, flow and the states of a bond](units-flow-and-bond-states.md), [heat as the source](heat-as-the-source.md),
[flow and heat](flow-and-heat.md) (rising and sinking, poles and arms, priming),
[structure over strength](structure-over-strength.md), [learned computation](learned-computation.md),
[decision 009](../decisions/009-no-context-in-cells.md).

---

## 1. A latch is a switch, not a flow

**The owner's correction.** A latch isn't something carrying activity. It is the traffic officer pointing the direction.
There may be no cars at the time, but the officer keeps pointing, so that when a car comes it goes that way. A switch,
and that is the basis of logic: controlling flow. A trace is a path through the switches left set.

So "latched" as "carrying flow right now" (from the earlier note) was the wrong meaning. If switches exist, a bond's
useful state is whether a switch points down it. §10 shows that switches may not be needed at all.

## 2. Completion is the sink

**The owner's idea.** When energy settles into a stable state, with every member reinforcing the others, that doesn't
mean those members stay active. What is left is a **trace**. So units come into the network, flow along the pathways,
and **fall into attractor states**, the basins. When a basin completes, the units' energy is settled, and **they
disappear**. If a basin can't complete, the units remain and cycle round.

**Why it works (assistant).**
- **It fits the definition of heat exactly.** Circulating units are trapped energy, energy that can't flow out. Settled
  energy is gone.
- **It fits Morowitz**: there is always a sink, and the sink is completion itself. Actions need not be the only way out.
- **It makes activity sparse for free.** Only what is unresolved stays active. Everything resolved goes quiet and becomes
  structure: roughly the difference between memory and attention.
- **It matches the Zeigarnik effect**, found almost a century ago: people remember unfinished tasks far better than
  finished ones, and unfinished things keep returning to mind.

**What it reverses.** The earlier note said a settled assembly is held on by units circulating round it. Here,
circulation means *unresolved* and settled means *quiet*. A completed memory isn't kept active. It is kept as a trace,
and it comes back when new units arrive, flow along its pathways, complete it again and drain away. **Remembering is
re-completing, not holding.**

**The risk.** Something that can never complete keeps its units cycling forever, as permanent heat, like a worry that
won't let go. That is where letting go, from the earlier notes, has to come in.

**An open question raised here.** If only the unresolved stays active, is something like "x is 5", held while waiting for
"x − y", held precisely because it is unfinished, because an operator is still waiting for it? (See §9.)

## 3. Stuck units grow, and growth is judged by energy

**The owner's account.** Units cycling in a basin that can't complete generate heat. Heat creates growth: they grow out,
trying to find connections that help them settle. Growth can let them leave the basin and come back, or escape. If the
new connections let the basin complete, they drop out. If not, they roll out and keep trying.

**How a new connection is judged.** It is conducive if it helps lower the energy. It is not conducive if it raises the
energy, by stopping completion or making it worse. That is a mechanical check made at the time. **It is exactly CTR-1
CON-1: a change is kept only if energy falls.** It isn't a new rule. It is the original one, applied to growth at the
moment it happens.

## 4. More units, more growth: flooding in a new form

**The owner's link to his earlier flooding idea.** Lowering sparsity in this model just means more units. More units
stuck in more places means more growth, in parallel, each doing its own job. When a path is found, the energy settles
and they all fall into the basin and drop out together. **Flooding and draining fall out of the units themselves**,
without loosening any threshold.

**Spawning.** The owner suggested that units that can't settle do two things: create growth on members, and possibly
spawn new members. In a later message, *spawn* was also used of units. **The assistant's pushback:** if stuck units create
more units, energy appears from nowhere, which undoes the fixed budget chosen precisely so that flooding can't run away.
Two honest versions keep the budget:

1. **The world keeps sending input**, so units pile up behind a basin that can't drain, like rain on a blocked river. The
   flood is real, but its energy came from outside.
2. **What spawns is members, not units**: growth makes new places for the existing units to spread into.

Unresolved. The first sketch, when there is one, should begin with bonds only, to keep one thing changing at a time.

## 5. The refractory period

**The owner's hunch.** Something must stop units piling up and doing exactly the same thing. Like neurons: after a neuron
fires, there is a period when it can't fire again. So a member that has just passed a unit can't take another
straight away. Units push each other onto different paths, explore different places, and sink into the basin at
different times.

**Support and consequences (assistant).** In neurons the refractory period is a large part of what keeps activity from
piling up and keeps signals travelling forwards. Here it spreads units out, which is what exploring needs. It has a
useful side effect. A channel one member wide can only pass so many units, so a busy narrow channel gets **congested**.
Congestion is heat, and heat grows the channel wider, like rivers widening where the flow is strong. It is a short-lived
state counted in moments, not a learnt number.

## 6. Rising and falling

**The owner recognised this as the rising and falling he described days earlier** ([flow and heat](flow-and-heat.md)).
Heat rises, and rising is growth, which usually means getting out of the basin to find connections to other basins,
other assemblies. Like a river trying to go downhill: first it floods, leaves its basin, finds a connection to a basin
further downhill, and that new pathway carries the flow. In his words: flooding happens because units are stuck and can't
sink, the flood finds connections to other basins, and when those new pathways are found, they all drain.

**Two corrections to keep it within the rules (assistant).**
- **A route gets easier by structure, not strength.** In a river, erosion deepens a channel with use. In this machine,
  deepening with use would be strength coming back. A route gets easier because more structure forms along it, such as a
  wider channel. The owner's congestion point is exactly this: a busy route gets heat and grows wider.
- **What makes one basin deeper than another?** The answer that fits: a deeper basin is an assembly that completes more
  easily, because it has more ways in. That is structure over strength again.

## 7. The landscape tilts

**The owner's picture.** What if basins aren't all equal, and the landscape shifts with context? Like a plate with a maze
on it and balls in the maze: tilt the plate from angle to angle, and that decides which balls move which way and which
paths they take down. What was a basin in one moment becomes high ground in the next. It depends on the tilt.

**Why it matters (assistant).** In a fixed landscape, a given input always drains the same way. With a tilting plate, the
maze is the learnt structure and stays put, but which way is downhill depends on the moment, so the same structure can
carry units to different places in different contexts.

**Its company.**
- In biology, **Waddington's landscape**: a developing cell rolls down a valley towards what it will become, and signals
  reshape the valleys.
- In neuroscience, the brain's attractor landscape is reshaped moment to moment by context and by chemicals such as
  dopamine, so the same circuit settles into different states.

**How it fits the project.**
- **Priming**, the warm layer, was always a kind of tilt: context making some assemblies easier to fall into.
- **Decision 009** said context isn't stored in cells. The tilt is where context lives instead.
- **The operator** in learned computation gets a home. "x − y" needs no wiring different from "x + y", only a different
  tilt over the same structure.

**Two cautions.**
- **The tilt must come from inside the machine.** If we set it, we are steering the computation by hand, which is
  puppeteering.
- **The tilt must change more slowly than the units flow**, or nothing settles: a slow layer shaping a fast one, the
  poles and arms of flow and heat.

## 8. The units are the gravity

**The owner's answer to "what tilts the plate?"** The units themselves, as if they had gravity.

**What it gives (assistant).** Nobody holds the plate. Wherever units gather, the landscape tips towards them, so the
context is simply where the energy currently sits: the machine's own state. It also replaces the distance field that the
earlier grid visuals had to supply. Two places where units are stuck pull on each other, so growth heads for other hot
spots: the owner's "hot places drawn together", with no global map.

**The danger.** Gravity alone collapses things: units attract units, the pull grows, everything clumps in one place, a
runaway like flooding. Stars only exist because gravity pulling in is balanced by pressure pushing out. The machine
already has both sides:

- **gravity** pulls units together and draws growth towards stuck regions;
- **the refractory period** pushes them apart: the pressure;
- **completion** drains them, so a clump that settles disappears.

Clumps form where there is heat, push outwards to explore, and vanish when they resolve.

**Reach.** If every unit pulls everything everywhere, that is global and expensive. If the pull only reaches through
existing structure, along bonds and a step or two beyond, it stays local and cheap, and the shape of what has been learnt
decides where the pull can be felt. The assistant's instinct is the second. Not yet settled.

## 9. Gravity is distributed

**The owner's correction to the collapse worry.** The trap is real only if gravity has a single centre. What if it is
**distributed**, with many basins that don't complete? And where do units from input go? Not all to the same place,
because gravity is spread through the topology, wherever things aren't resolved.

**What follows (assistant).** The landscape is a set of dips wherever something is open. The tilt at any spot is the
combined pull of all of them, strongest from whatever is nearest and most unresolved. **Input units disperse towards
whatever open question they are near: the unresolved things they might help complete.** That is relevance, or
attention, falling out of gravity.

**It is learned computation exactly.** When "x − y" is waiting, the open operator is an unresolved basin, so it pulls.
When "y" arrives, its units are drawn straight to it. **The waiting thing attracts what can finish it.** This also
answers §2's question: "x is 5" is held because it is unfinished, with an operator still waiting.

**What still holds.** Distributed doesn't remove clumping, it makes it local. Each open basin is a small collapse, and two
open basins close together would merge into one. So pressure and draining are still needed, everywhere at once. Open
basins also **compete for incoming units**: the one pulling hardest gets them first, a fair priority order. But a basin
that can never complete would pull forever and hog input, so letting go still matters.

**A visual** showed three open basins pulling units in, input arriving and dispersing to different basins, units kept
apart by the refractory rule, basins completing and draining, and the pull redistributing. It also showed an old trace of
switches leading to a settled basin, with a toggle for which wins when they disagree:

- **When the pull wins**, units attend to what is unresolved and ignore what was learnt.
- **When the switches win**, units follow the old trace and re-complete the settled basin, even with an open question
  pulling right beside it.

The rules in the visual were the assistant's, chosen to illustrate.

## 10. No switches: only the landscape

**The owner's idea, offered "not to be taken too seriously".** What if there are no latches or switches at all, only
the tilting, only the gradients, only the landscape moving? Picture the topology on a flat sheet, with parts of it able to
sink and rise in a third dimension, and units flowing down. The pathways, the bonds between members, are still there.
The only difference is that different pathways are taken depending on the tilt, on where gravity is pulling things
down to.

**It deserves to be taken seriously (assistant), because it makes the design smaller** (CTR-1 OPZ-3, minimality). Each
member has a height, and units roll downhill along whatever bonds exist. A switch was only ever "which way does the flow
go from here?", and here that is simply whichever neighbour is lowest. **The switch doesn't vanish. It becomes derived
from the landscape instead of stored on the member.** The bonds say which paths exist. The heights decide which is taken
right now. **Bonds are memory; the slope is context.**

**Where memory lives.** The assistant asked: when nothing is happening, is the landscape flat, or does it keep a shape? A
kept shape would be a number stored on members, which risks erosion, meaning strength. The owner's answer:

> Nothing has to be left behind. If a pathway from the top to the bottom worked the first time, and then things flatten
> out, and then it rises again in the same way, it would very likely take the same pathway, or a very similar one.
> Latches don't help with that. The pathways help with that. If there are no pathways that let the units roll down,
> pathways are forged, which is the growing.

**So:**
- **The same input in the same context** tilts the landscape the same way, so units roll down the same pathways. A route
  is reused because the channels are still there, not because a switch remembered it.
- **A different context** tilts it differently, so the same pathways carry units elsewhere: context-dependent routing,
  with nothing stored for it.
- **Where no pathway exists**, units are stuck, and growth forges one. *Forging, not erosion*: adding a pathway where
  there was none, not deepening one with use.
- **The landscape is flat when nothing is active.** All lasting memory is in which pathways exist. All context is in where
  the units are right now.
- **The state of the machine is where the energy currently is**: which basins are open and where units sit. That is real
  state, and it changes as units move. So the computation in the routing, the conservative-logic idea, survives without
  switches: routing comes from the landscape the units themselves shape.

**What shrinks.** Bonds, switches and units become **bonds and units**, with the landscape worked out from where the units
are. The bond states shrink to match:
- **candidate** and **settled**, for whether a bond has earned its place;
- **idle** and **strained**, for what is happening on it now.

"Latched" and "selected" have nothing left to describe.

**Ties.** When two pathways from a member slope downhill equally, nothing breaks the tie. The contract says not to guess
(CON-7), so the tie is held: units wait until something tips the slope. That is a natural place for context to do its
work.

## 11. What this changes in earlier notes

- [Units, flow and the states of a bond](units-flow-and-bond-states.md): "loops are persistence" is reversed (circulation
  is unresolved heat; settled is quiet, a trace). "Latches route flow" becomes "the slope routes flow". The bond state
  "latched" is dropped.
- [Heat as the source](heat-as-the-source.md): the phantom fits as one kind of unresolved basin, a source of pull. Heat
  spreading back from it is the same as the pull here.
- The language: latches had been deferred. They may not be needed at all.

## 12. Open questions

1. **What spawns**: input piling up behind a blocked basin, or new members as places to spread into? Units can't multiply
   on their own without breaking the budget.
2. **How the slope is worked out from the units**: what the pull of an open basin looks like, how it adds up, and how far
   it reaches. Local, through existing structure, is preferred.
3. **What completion takes, in units**: how many units a basin needs to complete, and where that number comes from without
   being tuned.
4. **Letting go**: how a basin that can never complete stops pulling, so it doesn't hog input forever.
5. **Timing**: the landscape must change more slowly than units flow. Does that come for free, since units gather slowly
   and drain on completion, or does it need something?
6. **Is the landscape really flat when quiet?** The owner's answer is yes. The test is whether a learnt route is reused
   when the same input arrives again, with no stored heights.
7. **Words and notation**: unit, basin, the pull, candidate, settled, idle, strained, and how the landscape is written in
   AMP, all to be agreed with the owner.
