# Architecture and regulation: many regions, each keeping its own balance

*A theory note, from a conversation with the owner (13 November 2026). Speculative: nothing here has been tested as a
whole. It brings the core idea (energy, heat, settling) back in at the level of the whole machine. Parts of it are
already supported by experiments C36–C58 (see [checkpoint 11](../checkpoints/11-thinking.md)); the rest is a direction.
Neuroscience is used as inspiration, not as a blueprint ([philosophy](../philosophy.md): inspired, not copied).*

---

## 1. The claim in one paragraph

Many of our hardest problems were solved not by a better rule inside one region, but by a **second region set up
differently and wired the right way**. That suggests moving up a level: designing the **architecture**, meaning many
copies of the same engine, each with a few declared settings, wired by a small vocabulary of connection types. What
animates that architecture is **heat**: each region has a **stasis** it tries to keep, any deviation is heat, and
**activity** (assemblies waking, projecting, propagating) is how a region works its way back. Regions differ in what
counts as settled for them and in how they respond, and through what they leave unresolved they keep each other going,
so the whole machine is **self-driven**, not only driven by what comes in.

## 2. Why move up to architecture

What the experiments already showed, without our calling it architecture:

| Problem | Not solved by | Solved by |
|---|---|---|
| Higher-level things lost their identity (C36–C42) | better pixel encodings | a region whose parts are the things it receives (C48, C53) |
| Made-up routes trusted (C45) | pricing tweaks | checking against experiences kept by **their own rule** (C46, C47) |
| Ambiguous half-seen views (C54) | a lower bar alone | **context sent down** from a higher region (C55, C56) |
| Joining routes never walked (C41) | a single region | the moving bar, start and end **held**, in the abstract region (C44, C51, C54) |

Each time the fix was a region with a different setting, wired in a particular way. So some of today's open problems
(busy scenes, attention, holding goals, acting, bigger assemblies over time) may also be architecture problems being
attempted inside one region with the wrong tools.

The brain makes this plausible. The cortex is strikingly uniform: much the same layered circuit repeated everywhere,
regions differing mainly in their connections, density, timing and how fast they learn. Around it are structures with
distinctly different jobs, for example: the hippocampus (learns fast from single experiences, replays them in sleep),
the prefrontal cortex (holds goals and things in mind over time), the thalamus (gates what reaches the cortex), the
basal ganglia (choose one action among competing ones), and small brainstem nuclei that broadcast signals such as
noradrenaline when things are surprising, changing how readily everything responds.

## 3. The aspects of an architecture

- **Composition:** which regions there are and how they are connected (section 4).
- **Configuration:** how each region is set up (section 5).
- **Timing:** each region's pace: how long a stretch of time it takes in at once, how often it rests. Higher areas of
  the brain integrate over longer stretches. Our bigger assemblies never formed (C57), perhaps because every region we
  built saw the same short window.
- **Modulation:** a global state, neither wiring nor a fixed setting, that changes settings on the fly. Heat lowering
  the bar everywhere is this kind of thing.
- **The boundary:** what comes in from the world (today, only seeing) and what goes out (acting: the missing half).
- **Growth** (later): the architecture itself changing over time.

## 4. Composition: a vocabulary for connections

Connections should come in a few kinds, so the architecture stays regular. Each connection has:

| Property | Choices | What we've seen |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | up · down · sideways (peers) · back to itself, delayed | up: what was recognised; down: context (C55); delayed to itself: transition memory |
| What it carries | **drives** (content: "this is what's here") or **modulates** (makes some things easier or harder) | our context from above modulated: it made naming cheaper but couldn't create sight (C55), as neuroscience describes most feedback |
| Layout | **map** (neighbours stay neighbours: "where" survives) or **pooled** (where is dropped) | C36 pooled recognised things and lost where they were (the lone cabin) |
| Fan | one-to-one · converging (many → one: covering more ground higher up) · diverging (one → many) | |
| Gating | always open, or opened and closed by something else | a candidate for attention (the thalamus) |
| Broadcast | from one place to everywhere | heat lowering the bar everywhere |

Most connections between cortical areas run both ways, so pairs of regions talking back and forth are the norm.

## 5. Configuration: what may differ between two copies of the same engine

Without changing the engine, a region may differ in:

- **What its parts are:** shapes (seeing) or the things it receives, each simply itself (C53).
- **How it keeps things:** by energy, generalising and compressing (cortex-like), or keeping each experience at once and
  letting it fade (hippocampus-like) (C47).
- **How fast it learns and how far back it remembers:** how often it rests; its memory window.
- **How much it can hold:** its size; its store's capacity.
- **How it responds to heat:** how far its bar can drop; whether it refuses to choose on a tie (C58).
- **How long a stretch of time it sees at once.**
- **What counts as settled for it, and which responses it has** (section 6). Probably the most important setting.

## 6. What animates it: heat, stasis and regulation

**Heat is distance from a stasis.** Each region has a state it settles towards. Any deviation is heat. **Activity** is
how heat shows up and how it moves: assemblies waking, projecting, propagating. Activity is the region's way of reducing
the deviation. The engine already works this way, with one stasis: *everything seen is explained as cheaply as
possible*. Misfit is the distance from it; rest cools it into structure; a question's gap lowers the bar and wakes
memories; a misfit sends a view back for a second look. Nothing moves when everything fits.

**Different regions, different stasis, different responses** (the owner's idea). A region's repertoire might include:
- **explain better** (re-describe, rest: learning);
- **push activity out** (the moving bar: thinking, joining);
- **quieten others** (inhibit or broadcast, so less comes in);
- **hold something steady** (a goal, the two ends of a question);
- **act on the world**;
- **rest**.

Each is a way back to its own stasis; activity is the common medium. Example (the owner's): a region whose stasis
includes "not too much activity for too long" responds to overload by inhibiting others, quietening what flows into it.
Modulation then arises as a **secondary effect** of a region reducing its own heat, not as a separate mechanism.

Brain parallels: neurons regulate their own activity towards a target level (homeostatic plasticity); a structure beside
the thalamus inhibits it to quieten what reaches the cortex (a leading account of attention); prolonged waking activity
builds pressure to sleep, and sleep is when memory is consolidated. So "activity for too long" leads naturally to rest,
which is exactly when our engine does its tidying.

**Self-driven.** In a many-region architecture, regions pass on what they couldn't settle: upward, what wasn't
explained; downward, the mismatch between expectation and arrival. Each works until its part settles. The regions keep
each other going through what they leave unresolved. (A well-known reading of the brain looks like this: predictions
flow down, surprises flow up, the whole works to reduce surprise.)

## 7. Conditions for it to work

- **Heat needs a source.** With nothing coming in and nothing unresolved, everything settles and goes quiet (fine for
  rest). A self-driven machine also needs inner sources: a question held, a goal, misfits left in memory to work
  through at rest, or the pull towards where it is learning fastest (curiosity, which must favour heat that *can* be
  resolved, not noise).
- **Heat needs a way to stop.** Some heat can never be resolved (noise, contradiction). Without damping, the machine
  would churn forever. Today's damping: the bar coming back up; experiences fading. Any architecture needs a way to give
  up on a gap as well as to close one.
- **One currency.** If heat flows between regions, it must mean the same everywhere. Today each region prices energy in
  its own terms (pixels in one, items and places in another). First design question: **what exactly flows between
  regions** (the unexplained part, the mismatch, the open gap) and how it is measured.

## 8. Discipline (so the architecture doesn't become crutches)

- **Every region is the same engine.** Regions differ only by a short, declared list of settings, each meaningful in the
  engine's own terms, never a tuned number.
- **Derive settings from position where possible** (for example, a region's time span grows with its height in the
  hierarchy), so most of the architecture follows from composition.
- **Connections come in the few kinds above.**
- **A stasis must be stated in the engine's own terms** ("everything explained", "nothing left open"). Something like
  "too much for too long" needs a principled measure, for example: energy has stopped falling despite activity (busy,
  but no longer learning), which is a natural signal to rest.
- **No special-purpose regions built to pass one test.** Architecture is the easiest place to hide puppeteering.

## 9. Open questions

- What flows between regions, and in what currency?
- What is each kind of region's stasis, stated in the engine's terms?
- Which responses belong to which regions, and how does a region choose among them?
- Is there one global heat (broadcast), or only local heat passed along connections, or both?
- Where do the inner sources of heat come from, without being hand-set?
- How do settings follow from position, so they needn't be chosen?

## 10. Possible first experiments

- **Rest by pressure:** a region rests when its energy has stopped falling despite activity, instead of every 98
  pictures.
- **Time span by height:** a third region over the abstract one, seeing longer stretches; do bigger assemblies (the
  aeroplane) form?
- **Quietening:** a region that inhibits its inputs when overloaded, tried on busy scenes (attention).
- **Holding:** a region whose stasis is "keep the goal active", replacing the hand-held ends of a question.
- **Map versus pooled:** the abstract region fed with "where" kept; does the lone cabin come back?

## 11. Where this would be built

A **studio** page in the sandbox: the architecture as the main view (regions as blocks, connections between them,
like a CAD assembly), clicking into a region opens its columns in 3D (`<an-columns>`), and running it shows heat and
activity flowing between regions, so a gap opening in one place and what lights up elsewhere can be watched.
Start with the two regions we have, laid out properly.
