# Theories

Longer theory notes: ideas about how the machine could work, worked out in conversation, before (or while) they are
tested. Speculative unless a note says otherwise; each says which experiments support it. The settled core is in
[philosophy](../philosophy.md) and [principles](../principles.md); what has been tested is in the
[checkpoints](../checkpoints/README.md).

**On terms:** the notes use "machine" and "automaton" freely. Those words are now retired. What runs AMP is **the
engine**, and everything it holds, knowledge and current state together, is **the memory**
([language terms](../../language/README.md#terms)). The notes are kept as written, as the record.

With how it meets C82-C85. |

| [Learnt logic: questions and answers](learnt-logic-qa.md) | Eight questions to the agent that did the learnt-logic work, its answers, and what they changed here: a candidate replacement for K (grow while misfit falls), why strengths were needed (random sampling), the two signs of heat being across time, and what picks the cell. |
| [Cells first](cells-first.md) | From bookkeeping to cells and segments (14 November 2026): columns keep memories apart across cells, segments within a cell; waking at two, completion, competition between whole memories; fitting by competition with re-settling where heat is left (in the core); composition by binding two cells per part; decomposition by the cut between fit and misfit; frames as a pattern, not a mechanism; work that follows what is active. With the evidence (C69-C78) and what is open. |
| [Learned computation](learned-computation.md) | The owner's step from transition memory to computation: the binding problem (what writes x, what reads it, what places 5 in "x − y"), answered by association with no registers or pointers: writing is bonding, reading is completion, an operator ties in and waits for what comes next, order comes from what was held against what arrives, and the answer is what the whole is associated with, primed before it lands. Computing is chaining learned associations. With the principle that composition and decomposition apply through transitions, as they did across a picture. Untested. |
| [Structure over strength](structure-over-strength.md) | Reinforcement as more structure, not stronger bonds: a memory is reinforced by new context being bonded to it (the owner's new-town example), every edge still binary. Structure gives robustness for free (a memory reached by many routes survives losing one), more ways to recall, context rather than a bare count, and speed (set operations, no weights). Fits Hebb and heat: nothing changes where things fit; gaps call for forming, misfits for breaking; learning grows outward from what is settled. The cost: richly connected mistakes entrench. Untested. |
| [How heat restructures](how-heat-restructures.md) | What the machine does with heat that changes structure, worked out in a question-and-answer exchange. Heat as two set differences (gap: form; misfit). Small gaps are filled by completion and ignored; context decides whether completion wins ("cat"/"cxt"/"cot"). New things form beside old ones; abstraction is forming the shared part, not cutting. Being wrong is settled by competition; breaking is local to the contact points and waits until one side has settled (timing, not strength, is the protection). Unsettled contests: look outward, let go, or contain; never invent. Confident mistakes are corrected only by variety and exploration. Trade-offs: chasing small heat, and forgetting (the owner's proposal: move cold structure to disk rather than delete). Untested. |
| [Nothing breaks](nothing-breaks.md) | A pivot from the founding premise: bonds never break. Worked out in a question-and-answer exchange ("cat"/"cot", crosstalk). Mistakes are outcompeted, not erased: gates have a limit, so new learning is new structure; what is in memory is decided by demand (votes from what is active, ranked with limited room, plus a floor), worked out fresh each moment with no stored number; what loses sinks to cold storage on disk, still connected but hard to reach. Two kinds of change remain: forming, and moving between warm and cold. Like a ledger. Supersedes the breaking sections of How heat restructures. Untested. |
| [Assemblies first](assemblies-first.md) | A second pivot: assemblies, not members, are the first-class citizens; members are pivot points assemblies join through. Bonds are stored with the gate that receives them (at most eight), so a stale bond leaves memory with the assembly that lost, not stuck to a busy member. Bonds and assemblies are both immutable: learning only forms new assemblies, so mistakes are isolated and go cold whole. Rank may decide where an assembly lives, never who wins. Deletion returns only as a storage policy for the coldest structure. Untested. |
| [Explainable by design](explainable-by-design.md) | Names the two halves of the method: AMP (Associative Memory Programming), the machine and its language, and AMX (Associative Memory Examination), everything for testing, probing, tracing, debugging and explaining it. Explainability is structural because development depends on it. Better than today's black boxes, not perfect (limits at scale, explained is not correct, transparency cuts both ways, alignment). What an explanation is in AMP, the record of changes, what the interpreter must do, AMX's phased test worlds and its honesty rules. The owner's vision: AMP as a new engineering discipline, intelligence that is programmed and examined rather than trained (the coral-reef robot). Why it feels harder than deep learning (binary, not continuous; comparing, not averaging), the kind of intelligence it might be, with pushback, and seeing its workings in words through AMT. Proposes CTR-1 GOL-5, OPZ-6, CON-20. |
| [Heat as the source](heat-as-the-source.md) | How an AMP automaton meets its world, worked through in one exchange: engine and automaton; don't stream input, let it look; a command line as its one interface, text in and out; learning commands from CLI feedback and the echo (imitation from heat); from nothing to something, and why noise and invented set points were rejected; the dark-room problem; the phantom assembly as a declared imbalance; latches as the fast part that settles over the static frame of bonds; seed versus noise; heat spreading back from the phantom (source) to a key (sink), closing Morowitz's cycle; the lattice as the medium. Ends on heat as the source and the machine as its medium. Sketches a first test. Untested. |
| [Units, flow and the states of a bond](units-flow-and-bond-states.md) | Curiosity read as topology and flow: unsettled regions grow inwards and outwards, pulled towards other clusters (slime mould, lightning, rivers); only holes that block flow get filled. A gap is completion that can't finish. Assemblies as channels, composition as the bridge, decomposition as the shared trunk. The bar of two and structural thresholds; flooding then draining as annealing. Energy as a fixed budget of units that move, never copy, so flooding is impossible by construction; loops are persistence; latches route flow (conservative logic). One kind of bond in states: candidate, settled, latched, idle, strained. Maps onto learned computation; the test is reuse. Untested. |
| [The tilting landscape](the-tilting-landscape.md) | Completion is the sink: units fall into basins and drain when they complete; what can't complete keeps cycling as heat and grows (Zeigarnik). Growth judged by energy falling (CON-1). More units as flooding; the refractory period spreads units and widens congested channels. Rising and falling. The landscape tilts with context (the maze on a plate; Waddington), and the units are the gravity, distributed over every unresolved basin, so input disperses to the open questions it can finish. No switches: units roll downhill along pathways; bonds are memory, the slope is context, the landscape is flat when quiet. Untested. |
| [After completion](after-completion.md) | Written to take away (21 November 2026). The engine keeps things only as activity, so holding both keeps what was read and blocks new input; reading a stream needs a hold to end and something to remain. What C103 (reading with bonds only) and C104 (completion drops out; one member fewer after) found: dropping out for a moment doesn't end it, and a lower threshold restarts the word from its own leftovers. The owner's proposal: a completed assembly drops out and leaves its members primed, not counted; primed members come on more easily and reset once activated; unexpected input turns on only its primed members (decided). Open questions, and notes for an agent picking it up. Untested. |
| [Sequence memory and state memory](sequence-and-state.md) | Written as a handoff (21 November 2026). The owner's answer to After completion, built on branch `l1a-reading`: reading with bursts and bonds (sequence memory, L1A); motifs cast into assemblies in a community of their own (state memory, L1B), with reused members, gates and competition. What C105 found about holding through noise and changing state: short assemblies inside long ones can't be beaten, and noise can carry a word's context through a burst. Open questions, starting with whether nested assemblies cooperate. Partly tested (C105). |
| [Categories from context](categories-from-context.md) | How "animal" forms in AMP. Implicit: alternatives grow in many frames, their recurring intersection forms an unnamed category (the distributional hypothesis, from alternatives and intersection, one sentence at a time), fuzzy first, then sharpening. Explicit: the word joins the same alternatives and linking sentences, then is grounded by overlapping the category. Structure grows in many directions (up, down, sideways, across frames, diagonally, through time, inwards): a web, not a tree. Why it could be quick, and an AMX test with predictions and what could fool us. Untested. |
| [Understandable by design](understandable-by-design.md) | Companion to Explainable by design. Explainability answers *why* it did something; understandability answers *what* it knows. The structure is the knowledge, so tools that read it let people and agents go into its mind: an open box, not artefacts generated afterwards. Human in the loop: inspect both the problem and the solution. First use case: learning a new domain by navigating the structure built from its documents, with links back to sources (a target for the AMX ladder, not near-term). Honest limits, tests, and draft CTR-1 GOL-6 and OPZ-7. |
| [Procedures from states and transitions](procedures-from-states-and-transitions.md) | How AMP can hold methods, not only patterns. Frames as a chassis with swappable parts; procedures as frames chained by transitions; a super-assembly as the frame of the whole, with switching, overlap and resuming by competition. A population with room for one as a register (holding, reading by key, writing by competition), which is where latches went. Loops keyed by the world; counting as a memorised chain advanced by arrivals, not held states; routines and subroutines as composition, at different paces by depth; returning to the caller. Learnt by acting. A hand-written first test with predictions. Untested. |
| [Content and spatial memory](content-and-spatial-memory.md) | The owner's idea of memory addressed both by content and by place: a huge two-dimensional landscape of text that an automaton roams, reading and writing like ink that can't be crossed out; content as wormholes to places, places priming content; working memory and episodic memory as the same thing; variables as content finding a place and the place giving its neighbours; column arithmetic as movement in space. What it gives (instances, indirection with structure, order without a program counter), how it meets earlier threads (the notebook, moving with commands, sinking), prior art to borrow from, open questions, what would count as faking, and a first test (adding numbers longer than any seen). Then, as a transcript of how the thinking moved on: placing input instead of streaming it; space, time and content, with everything bound to coordinates whose dimensions a program declares; and the automaton as the substrate itself, a material of content and coordinate bonds with energy flowing through it, so attention is only where the energy is. Untested. |
| [Learned competition and computation across addresses](learned-competition-and-addresses.md) | Side notes. Competition spaces learned rather than global: attraction from what stays the same, competition where things vary, placed at the place, tied to the type, between bindings rather than values. Computation learned over addresses: registers holding only activity, attention learned by heat, generalising by indirection. With what C137 to C139 found and what is open, and several heads (after the Turing machine's head), not to be taken too seriously yet. Partly sketched. |
| [Candidate energy rules](candidate-energy-rules.md) | Three candidate rules for how heat moves activity, as hypotheses to choose between, each replacing the search still in the experiment's code in C142 and C143: (A) a fixed budget of units flowing to the heat; (B) heat lowering the bar around hot cells, with competition between rival assemblies picking; (C) settling spreading out from the gap. What any candidate must do, C143's four lessons (attention goes to heat; rivals settle separately; finished only when no heat is left; ambiguity branches), what each predicts, its risks, a side-by-side comparison, and the assistant's recommendation (B first).
