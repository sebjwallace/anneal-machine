# Flow and heat: the original premise

A philosophical theory, from the owner (15 November 2026), recorded because it is close to the premise the project
started from and had not been written down. It is an analogy and a lens, not a mechanism to copy literally: the
pictures of rivers, factories, dots and lines say what the machine is for and how it should behave, not what its cells
look like. The last two sections say how it meets the experiments so far and what it asks of the machine.

## 1. Energy that flows, and energy that can't

The machine is about the flow of energy. Where energy flows well, little of it is trapped. A healthy river is the
first picture: the water moves, and nothing builds up.

A factory is the second. Cogs, cranks and pistons are all connected. When everything works, energy passes through the
machinery smoothly. It is never perfectly efficient, and there is always some loss, but within its constraints the
whole is balanced and coherent. It is healthy.

Then something wears. One cog warps, so its teeth no longer mesh cleanly, and heat builds up where they grind. Two gears
that relied on turning at exactly the same speed drift apart, and the difference becomes tension. **Heat is the
symptom:** of stress, misfit and malfunction. It is a sign of energy that cannot flow.

## 2. The medium: a living topology

In the machine, what the energy flows through is the whole of its active assemblies: the ones that have settled and
persist, changing only when something forces them to. Seen from far above, they are dots and lines, a topology. When
they fit together like a good puzzle, energy moves through them freely.

It doesn't move like a river, which only runs downhill. It moves in and around the assemblies, along whatever the dots
and lines allow, and the topology itself is not fixed. As energy changes in one place, the assemblies there may change
shape. Often that is fine: the assemblies around them accommodate the change, and the flow goes on. Sometimes it isn't:
the change leaves its neighbours not fitting, their energy rises, and they reconfigure in turn, changing which
assemblies are active, and so on outwards, until the whole settles back down together.

Heat builds up where the topology gives energy nowhere to go. A dot with no line onward is a dead end: energy arrives
and stops. A small closed loop is another: energy swirls round it and can't get out. There may be many such shapes, and
each is a symptom of misfit. They are not drawings of cells and bonds. They show that the system of active assemblies
is a medium of flow, and that misfit is wherever that medium blocks or traps it.

## 3. Heat spreads

Stress doesn't stay where it started. In the factory it passes to the machines connected to the faulty one, and in
time causes faults there, which make heat of their own. In the machine, the cascade above is the same thing: one
assembly that no longer fits heats its neighbours, and they theirs. Left unresolved, heat cascades, and the whole
degrades.

## 4. Essential and accidental energy

Total energy is not the problem. All the energy in the system is accounted for, and a working system needs energy to
work. The problem is the waste. **There is essential energy, which the work needs, and accidental energy, which it
doesn't. The accidental energy is the heat.**

## 5. Patching: heat baked into structure

One response to a fault is to patch it. Tinker with the gears that don't mesh, or build something on top that manages
the fault, so energy flows again for now. But the patch is extra machinery. It has to be driven, so it adds energy of
its own, and it brings more ways to fail. The underlying problem is still there.

In heat terms, a patch turns heat into structure without resolving it. The heat isn't gone. It is held in the
structure, encapsulated and masked: a dead end or a loop built in. Inefficiency has been baked in, and the accidental
energy is still there. It just doesn't flow any more.

**The proper fix** is the opposite. Repair the machine, replace the worn part, oil it, look after it, and where possible
improve and upgrade it. These are the sustainable solutions, because they let energy flow, and that stops heat building
up and causing faults, stress and degradation.

## 6. Heating and cooling

Heat that can't flow causes change at two depths. First, the **active assemblies** change: activity shifts, assemblies
take other forms or give way to others, quickly and without building anything. If that doesn't let the energy flow,
the **structure beneath** changes: the bonds loosen and re-form, slowly. As a population, assemblies that are hot
rearrange and re-form, jiggling like atoms, until things make sense and click into place. Then they settle.

The two depths are also two kinds of bond. Picture a field of poles, each with arms that swing round it and reach out
to the arms of its neighbours. The **poles are the settled structure**, slow memory: "cat", "man", "x", "5", things
seen and settled over and over, which never need relearning. The **arms are the fast part**: activity and quick
bonds that turn on the poles and pin things together for now ("the" goes with the duck; "x" is "5"). Reconfiguring
activity is the arms swinging, so the fast part dances on the slow part and works off the back of it. A pot on the heat
is the same picture from the side: at the top, where it is hot, things move quickly and keep re-forming; where they
lock together into a sound, lower-energy form, they cool and sink into the slower, calmer depths, and become poles
themselves.

When they settle, **the heat that was there becomes structure**: the bonds. Bonds are the after-effects of cooling, the
artifacts it leaves. If whatever caused the heat happens again, there is now structure that lets things reconfigure
and cool straight away. The cycle is heating and cooling. Faced with a heat it hasn't met before, the machine has to
find a configuration that drops it into a lower-energy state, and when energy can flow again, the heat turns into
structure. Energy is not created or destroyed, only moved and transformed. The dynamics of the machine are the
movement and transformation of energy.

## 7. Sinking and rising: new against old

What decides whether something sinks is whether it fits what is already settled. Suppose the slow depths know that
cats walk, run and sit. Then, in the fast surface, the machine reads that cats fly. New and old disagree, there is heat
between them, and one of them has to give way. Context may settle it (it is only a story), but until something does,
the heat holds the new thing at the surface. It cannot sink.

Now suppose instead it already knows that humans and whales are mammals, and hears for the first time that cats are
too. That is new structure, but it agrees: cats walk like humans, humans are mammals, and the new link only fills a
gap between dots that were already there. It is literally connecting the dots. Energy that had nowhere to go now has a
line to flow along, and that is where insight, ideas and understanding come from, and the feeling of coherence and
clarity. Such a link settles at once, sinks, and slots into place, like a falling piece in a game of Tetris finding the
gap it fits.

So **sinking is agreement and rising is disagreement.** Heat between new and old keeps the new at the surface; fit
lets it fall.

People feel the rising as discomfort: confusion, unrest, sometimes anger. New information against old disturbs the
whole web of assemblies. One way out is a rationale: a new belief, a different point of view, a story that lets
everything make sense again and the energy flow. Sometimes that is right. But often it is the patch of section 5: not
fixing the machine, but adding structure to it so the cogs turn again. **The failure** is heat that turns into structure
only to be masked: held as heat inside the structure. The hope is that such structure must, sooner or later, heat up
again, and that the heat melts the patch away, so things reconfigure and settle into their natural place. Then heat
drops in the structure and in the flow alike: the structure turns back into energy and flows out again.

Not everything settles. Some things sink only halfway, half making sense. And heat keeps arriving from a world that
will never fully make sense. The contemplative traditions (Taoism, Buddhism) meet a paradoxical world by accepting it,
and are freed from it. The machine needs its own way to let go, or it would buckle under heat that never stops coming.
In the pot, that is **evaporation**: what can't settle leaves, rather than being forced into place.

## 8. The world, mirrored, and the purpose

The world outside isn't perfect. Its systems are misaligned, conflicted and stressed: they hold heat. When the machine
takes them in, it learns them. It builds their structures and assemblies inside itself, so the heat out there is
mirrored in here.

That is its purpose. **It is an annealer.** It is meant to find what cools that heat internally, and then, because
what it holds is knowledge of the world, perhaps help cool it externally too. Because heating and cooling are built into
it, it may see things differently from anything else. It takes in the structure and the heat of the world, reconfigures
and cools them inside, and reflects that cooler structure back out, so energy can flow better again. Its cooled
structure stands for solutions: the machines fixed properly, not patched.

## 9. How this meets the experiments (C82-C87)

- **The three shapes of heat** (C84) read as places where the medium blocks flow. Something there that nothing holds:
  energy arriving with no line to take it. Something held that isn't there: a line leading to nothing, a dead end
  built into structure. Something held twice: two routes for the same energy, neither taken.
- **Masked heat is measurable.** In C84 the core explained every one-off input as fragments of old memories, so
  "something there that nothing holds" fell to zero almost at once. That looks like flow, but "something held that
  isn't there" never fell: the patched one-offs keep reaching for what never arrives. That is section 5, measured.
- **Heat spreads.** With one-offs sharing the objects' columns, their bonds joined object to object until half of any
  object brought back the whole volume.
- **Melting patches.** Fading a bond each time it claims what isn't there cut that chaining by two-thirds, but the core
  patched again at once, because it bonds the moment anything doesn't fit (C84).
- **Bonding on cooling** (C85). A memory may explain an input only if it explains as much as it claims that isn't
  there (the old bill's prices); one-offs stay loose and are cleared when cells run short. Where one-offs had columns
  of their own, structure stayed flat, heat held in structure fell about twentyfold, recall stayed exact, and the
  one-offs' heat passed through unexplained. Where they shared the objects' cells, the chaining stopped, but the objects
  lost cells to them. And the bill forbids recall from a small cue.
- **Poles and arms** (C86). A slow volume and a fast one, the fast one fed by the slow one's winners: each kind of
  knowledge survived in its own volume, which one volume can't manage, but read together they disagreed, because
  nothing made them settle against each other.
- **Sinking by fit** (C87). The deep's own settling says whether a new link connects its dots, and gating on it kept
  "cat flies" out. But settling spread to everything connected, so every dot was joined already: no near or far.
- **Lowering the bar** (C88, primitive). Letting the deep's settling through one round at a time from each end gave
  the aha a place: the fronts met in a small lit region. Near and far showed only faintly, and the size of the aha
  wasn't graded yet; a primed deep may change that.
- **Both depths of change.** C82 to C85 all work on the slow depth, the bonds. The fast depth, active assemblies
  shifting and cascading without changing a bond, needs assemblies that persist, which the core doesn't have yet
  ([persistent assemblies](persistent-assemblies.md)).

## 10. What it asks of the machine

- **Bond on cooling, not on heating.** Bonds formed while hot are loose; they freeze only when the same thing returns
  and fits.
- **Try the fast change before the slow one.** Heat should first move activity, and only reach the bonds if activity
  can't settle it.
- **Fast bonds work on slow structure.** What is quick ties together what is settled, without relearning it, and only
  what settles in the fast part sinks into the slow. A slow volume and a fast one may be two ends of a spectrum, not
  two kinds of thing.
- **Structure that holds heat must heat up again,** so a patch can melt. No structure may be exempt from the measure.
- **Not all heat becomes structure.** What never settles flows through and out.
- **Sink what fits; hold up what conflicts; let go of what never settles.** A new link sinks when the settled structure
  already connects its two ends; it stays at the surface while it conflicts; it evaporates if it never settles, rather
  than being patched into place with a rationale.
- **Judge by accidental energy, not total.** Structure that carries flow is not a cost to be cut; structure that holds
  heat is.
