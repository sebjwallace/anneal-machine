# C169 · Two rows, by hand: hypothesis

Step 3 of [schema and spatial memory](../../../docs/theories/schema-and-spatial-memory.md) §9: the pieces from
[C165](../C165-a-hand-over-on-an-arrival/findings.md) to [C168](../C168-a-latch/findings.md) put together for a two-pass
procedure, long multiplication's two rows, all by hand. In [C163](../C163-the-second-row/findings.md) the second row
needed the experiment to move the focus to its start and give the cue again. Here the memory gets there itself.

## The procedure

Held by one procedure cell, started by the cue:

1. **The first row** (the holding frame, as learned in C157/C158): hold the multiplier's units digit, work the top
   number leftwards, carrying the sign along.
2. **When it runs out** (the arrival, from competition as in C166): a hand-over to **the jump**.
3. **The jump** brings the focus to **the place one to the left of where the procedure started.** That's the start latch
   from C168 (the cue's place, held while the procedure is held), plus one relation between places ("one to the left"),
   so it's a small spatial composition, not the experiment moving the focus.
4. **The second row** (the second-row frame from C163): hold the multiplier's tens digit, read the top number one place
   to the right, write onto the row below, its carries in the row above it.
5. **When it runs out:** Done, and the procedure lets go (C167).

Every hand-over is keyed on the procedure, the arrival and a sparse sample of the frame that just ended (C165).

## What's given (declared)

The frames, the procedure, the hand-overs, the jump's relation and Done, all by hand. Architecture: the clock with the
arrival from competition, frames held by their own running, holding (pick-up, held value), the start latch, the focus
kept through hand-overs, letting go by competition. The page (the encoder), **including a 0 carried in above where the
second row starts**, as the first row's 0 is given above where it starts. The facts. **Removed:** C163's restart (the
experiment putting the focus at the second row's start and giving the cue again).

## Arms

1. **Two rows:** 200 long multiplications, 2- to 5-digit tops by 2-digit multipliers.
2. **Jump to the start itself** (control): the jump goes to where the procedure started, not one to the left. The
   second row should then be one place out, so it fails.

## Predictions (fixed before running)

1. **Two rows:** in every multiplication, the first row right, one jump landing one column left of the start, the second
   row right and in the right place, and the memory going quiet by itself.
2. **Jump to the start itself:** the first row right. The second row fails in nearly every case, because its reads are
   one place out: it holds the units digit (not the tens) and reads the top number one place out.
