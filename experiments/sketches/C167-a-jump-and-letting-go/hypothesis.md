# C167 · A jump, and letting go: hypothesis

The second small step from [schema and spatial memory](../../../docs/theories/schema-and-spatial-memory.md) §9, with
letting go, which [C165](../C165-a-hand-over-on-an-arrival/findings.md) and
[C166](../C166-an-arrival-from-competition/findings.md) left open. Everything is still written by hand, and nothing is
learned. The [crutch register](../../crutch-register.md) gets the new pieces.

## The question

Can a procedure **jump** the focus to a place picked out by something larger than one step from where it is (here,
the place where the procedure started), and can a procedure **let go** when its last way of working ends, both by the
memory's own completion and competition?

## The pieces

- **Binding where a procedure starts.** When the cue to begin is given at a place, the fixed binding rule forms one
  gate: *the procedure, with a jump, brings on that place.* This is the smallest spatial anchor bound to a procedure,
  "this procedure, over the region starting here", from the theory note §3. It's architecture, of the same kind as the
  rule that writes digits.
- **Three ways of working in a chain, held by the procedure.** A: the reading frame, working the sum leftwards. J: a
  jump, a single step that, with the procedure held, brings on the bound start place (and lets the old focus go). C: a
  copying frame (as C165's B, but leftwards), copying the answer onto the row below from the start. Hand-overs on
  arrivals, each keyed on the procedure, the arrival, and a sparse sample of the frame that just ended (as C165 and
  C166, with the arrival from competition as C166).
- **Letting go by competition.** What follows the procedure is either the procedure again (it holds itself) or Done.
  The two are alternatives, so they're rivals. Done is backed by the procedure, the arrival, and a sample of C, the last
  frame, so it wins only when C ends. Then the procedure isn't renewed, and the memory goes quiet.

## Arms (200 additions, 3 to 6 digits, each in a fresh memory except arm 4)

1. **Jump and let go:** as above.
2. **No start bound** (control): the binding rule off. The jump should bring on no place, so C never runs.
3. **No letting go** (control): no Done. The procedure should stay held at the end, as in C165 and C166.
4. **A second sum in the same memory** (to expose a weakness): two 3-digit additions one after the other, at different
   columns, in one memory, each given its own cue. The bound start is permanent structure, not a temporary latch, so the
   second sum's jump is expected to bring on **both** start places.

## The ledger

**Given:** the three frames, the procedure, the hand-overs and Done, all by hand; the binding rule for where a
procedure starts, frames held by their own running, the focus kept through a stall and hand-over, the two alternatives
after phase 5, and the alternatives after the procedure (procedure or Done) (architecture); the page; the facts. **The
engine:** everything after each cue.

## Predictions (fixed before running)

1. **Jump and let go:** in every sum, A right, then a jump straight back to the start column (one jump, a few moments,
   no walking), C copies the answer right, and the procedure lets go: everything goes quiet by itself.
2. **No start bound:** A right, the jump brings on nothing, nothing is copied, and the procedure (still held) is the only
   thing left on.
3. **No letting go:** as arm 1, but the procedure stays held at the end.
4. **Second sum in one memory:** the first sum is fine. The second sum's jump brings on both its own start and the first
   sum's, and its copy goes wrong. If so, a procedure's bound place needs to be temporary, going when the procedure
   lets go, and the engine has nothing temporary yet.
