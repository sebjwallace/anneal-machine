# C187 · The final adding: hypothesis

Written before the run, after a build check (`sanity.js`: a hand-written final-adding way of working, kept in the
memory, settles 10 of 10 final-adding pages with the sum right). Date: 2026-11-28.

## The question

C186's relation hubs learned the second row in every seed. But the second row is close kin to times one digit. The owner
asked for a stress test, to see how general these abstractions get. The next lesson in school order, the final adding of
long multiplication, shares less. It's adding, but it reads the two rows several rows apart and writes the sum far
below, while the adding lesson reads just above where it writes. Some relations the memory has distilled should carry
over (the carry written one left of where it's read; the sign written along), and others shouldn't (where the reads sit
relative to the answer).

## The setup

- **One memory per arm and seed:** adding and times one digit (as C176), then the second row by C186's relation hubs
  (the same seed for both arms, so both arms start from the same memory), then the final adding.
- **A final-adding lesson page** (the encoder): the question with both rows done (the teacher's, C170). The first row's
  signs are left off and a "+" is put under the first column, as on paper before the sum. The 0 carried in for the sum
  is given. The sum and its carries are the target. Four worked examples (seeded). The sign "+" is in view at the start.
- **Two arms, three seeds each,** with everything else as C186:
  - **no hubs:** C185's anchored, widening search, on the final adding;
  - **hubs by relation:** C186's completion.
- **Tests,** in the same memory: 200 new final additions, and 200 new second rows afterwards, to check the new lesson
  hasn't disturbed the old one.

## Predictions

1. **The start brings up adding** (seen with "+"), not the second row or times one digit (seen with "×").
2. **Both arms find the final adding harder than the second row.** It's far from adding: every read and both writes must
   move, only the sign read stays, and most moves are long (four or five rows).
3. **Hubs by relation do better than no hubs,** but by less than on the second row. The relations that would help (the
   carry written one left of where it's read) are shared widely. But the most widely shared relation between the answer
   and the reads is adding's own, which is wrong here, so completion will often pull parts to the wrong place. Expected:
   hubs by relation 2 of 3 seeds, no hubs 0 or 1 of 3.
4. **The second row stays right** (200 of 200) in every arm where it was learned in the background, since the view keeps
   the lessons apart.
5. **Risks:** completion misleading the search into a wrong basin; the coincidence; everything stuck.
