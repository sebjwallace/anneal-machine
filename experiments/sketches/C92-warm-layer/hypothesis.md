# C92 · The warm layer: hypothesis

A rough sketch, not preregistered. From the owner's picture of priming: what is active is only the surface; underneath,
assemblies that were woken but didn't surface stay warm, ready to bubble up when one more input tips them. RM-10 names
the same thing from the other side: a frame needs a way to **hold a variable open**, the possibilities held as
possibilities, "not blended, not guessed", until a cue picks one.

**Where the core stands.** After "ABCD" and "ABEF" (and the cut that leaves "AB" as the shared part), "AB" alone
brings back both "CD" and "EF". Then they are dropped: the moment before keeps only what was seen (CON-8). The next
input, a lone "E", finds nothing: one cell can't reach the bar of two, so the column bursts and takes a fresh cell, a
new "E" every time.

**The one change.** What a moment brings back but doesn't see is held warm for the next moment. A warm cell adds to the
segments it reaches, as an active cell does, but it can never make a segment fire on its own: at least one of the
count must be an active cell. Warmth tops up; it never wakes. Warmth is a set of cells carried over, like the moment
before (CAP-12), counted per segment and never pooled in a cell (CON-9).

**Hypothesis.** With the warm layer, "AB" then "E" brings back "EF" from that single cell, reusing the learnt "E";
"CD" stays quiet; "E" with nothing before it wakes nothing; warmth is gone after a gap.

**Givens (declared).** Warmth lasts exactly one moment. Warmth is what was brought back, not what was seen. A blank
input separates the lessons, so no transitions are learnt between them. Four cells per column, each lesson three times.

**What would count against it.** Warmth waking too much (things that were never warm, or warmth alone firing
segments), or warmth that never fades.
