# C38 · Findings (40 walks; 80 gives the same picture)

**What it holds.** 32 things: single views, and stretches of 2–5 views. 8 walks are held whole as one thing
(e.g. stream → oak → junction B → bridge). The rest are held only as pieces: a walk is kept whole only when that pays.

**1. Walked routes from their two ends.** When the walk is held as one thing: filled in exactly, 9 of 9. When it isn't:
5 of 31. Overall 14 of 40. So recall-by-two-ends works exactly as the owner described, and only for routes stored whole.

**2 and 3. Never walked together (10 pairs, incl. bench → oak).** A real route: 0 of 10, gap given or chosen.
It fills a few steps from each end with real places and then blurs (e.g. bench → gate → (blur) → pond → junction B → oak).

**Why.** Nothing the engine holds contains both ends, so the "meet at the top" has nowhere to happen: there is no
higher thing above walks, because a strip only ever holds one walk, so walks never appear together and nothing builds
regions from them. And two pieces each pinned by one end are not drawn to join at their shared junction: the junction
is hidden, and hidden places cost nothing but earn nothing, so joining up is not rewarded. Same flat ground as C37.

**Cost.** Rest grows slow on strips: 40 walks 8 s, 80 walks 30 s, 100 walks didn't finish in 5 minutes.

**Result.** Routes as assemblies over time work for recall: stored walks come back whole from two cues by the engine's
own completion. Novel routes don't: the missing pieces are (a) something above walks (where walks sharing a junction
are held together), and (b) a reason for pieces to join where they meet. Engine unchanged.
