# C178 · Seeing more at the start: hypothesis

Written before the run, after the build check (`sanity.js`: times one digit and the second-row way of working, each kept
with the view of one page of its kind, were brought up on 10 of 10 new pages of their own kind, all right). Date:
2026-11-27.

## The question

In C177, the memory learned the second row but couldn't tell when a page called for it. All it noticed at the start was
the sign, and a first row and a second row both start under "×". So from the start it brought up times one digit every
time (0 of 200). If it notices more of the page at the start, can it tell them apart, and does that change how it learns?

## The setup

C177's run (one memory, school order, heat-guided adapting, tie-break arm), with three changes:

- **What's in view at the start** (the encoder, as since C159): at each place within reach of the start, a digit, a sign
  or nothing. Only the kind of thing is seen, not which digit, so pages of the same kind look alike whatever their
  numbers (an abstraction by the encoder, declared). This is given with the cue, except when an offer is tried (C177's
  fix).
- **Kept with it:** when a page settles, the way of working that did the work is kept with the view, as one assembly of
  everything seen and the frame cell. Its support is how much of that view is seen now, less how much isn't. Frames kept
  with views that share things are rivals (rivals from place, C145, with company from these assemblies only, C146).
  C176's link from the start through the sign stays as it was.
- **Four second-row worked examples** instead of two (C172's two, then two more, seeded). Adapting starts from the way
  of working that started the work, if any.

## Predictions

1. **Adding and times one digit** are learned as in C176 and C177 (about 12 runs for times one digit).
2. **Second row, first example:** as C177. Times one digit is brought up, fails, and is adapted (about 200 runs). The
   result may again be the coincidence (the carry read in the wrong place).
3. **Second example:** the start brings up the first example's result, whose view matches best. If it's the
   coincidence, it fails, and adapting starts from it and needs one change. **Far fewer runs than C177's 208.**
4. **Third and fourth examples:** if both the coincidence and the right one are kept, with the same view and one copy
   each, they tie. The first formed (the coincidence) wins, fails, and the right one settles as an offer. By the fourth
   example the right one has more copies and is brought up first, and settles.
5. **Tests, in the same memory:**
   - adding 200 of 200;
   - times one digit 200 of 200;
   - **second rows from the start: 200 of 200** (C177: 0);
   - second rows with the learned one offered: 200 of 200.
6. **Risk:** pages of one kind don't all look alike at the edge of reach (shorter and longer numbers), so the views
   match only partly. If a times page ever brings up the second-row way of working (or the other way round), it shows
   in the test's "what the start brought on".
