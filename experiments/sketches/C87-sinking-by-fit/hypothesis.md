# C87 · Sinking by fit: hypothesis

Written 15 November 2026, from the owner's picture of sinking and rising ([flow and
heat](../../../docs/theories/flow-and-heat.md), section 7). C86 left open how a fast volume and a slow one settle into
one answer, and how what settles in the fast one sinks into the slow one (QST-17). The owner's answer: what agrees with
the settled structure sinks, slotting into place like a Tetris piece; what conflicts stays at the surface; what never
settles evaporates. A link agrees when it connects the dots: the settled structure already joins its two ends some
other way ("cats are mammals", since cats walk like humans and humans are mammals). "Cats fly" has no such route.

**The rule (model.md):** a link the fast volume has settled sinks into the slow volume if settling from each of its
ends there brings back something in common. Otherwise it evaporates from the fast volume.

**Hypothesis:** links that connect the dots sink and links that don't stay out, so the slow volume learns "cat mammal"
and "dog mammal" and not "cat flies", where a slow volume that learns everything takes in all of them.

**Two cases chosen to test the rule's limits:** "fish mammal" (false, but connected: fish swim like whales, whales are
mammals) and "bird walks" (true, but not connected in this small world).

**A risk:** the core's settling has no limit on how far it spreads (RM-4, RM-5's one global level). If it reaches the
whole connected world from any cue, every connected link is "known" already, and the test can only say "connected or
not".
