Never-seen sentences ("the sun warms the river", "the fire heats the sea", "the rain feeds the plant"), held, 12
sentences: each explained by **one placement** of a constant ("the … warms the" and so on), with the two words in the
variables described as themselves.

## What it shows
- **Constants come from two sentences and one rest.** From just "the sun warms the sea" and "the sea warms the air",
  comparison found `the … warms the`: exact, at once, with no counting. With 12 sentences, it found the constant of
  every frame (`warms`, `heats`, `feeds`, three sentences each) and the one they all share (`the … the`). One rest
  more than halved the energy of what was kept. This is the owner's point, borne out at small scale: comparison sees
  sameness directly.
- **But only when the sentences are held.** Kept by energy, nothing is ever kept from a single sight (as C60 found), so
  there is nothing to compare. Few-shot learning needs what is seen once to be held, as experiences are, and to take
  part in rest.
- **Frames with variables, and types, didn't form.** The constants were found, but not "the · warms the ·" as a frame
  whose variables group sun, sea, fire. Each pairing of subject and object was seen once, so a frame listing them
  doesn't pay yet; and the engine keeps lists of fillers, not types (the glossary's point). A never-seen sentence is
  recognised by its constant, but its words aren't recognised as fitting a variable.
