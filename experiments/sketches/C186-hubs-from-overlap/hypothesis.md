# C186 · Hubs from overlap: hypothesis

Written before the full run. Date: 2026-11-28. **Disclosed: one arm-seed was seen first.** A check that the code runs, on
"hubs by relation, seed 1", went through to its end: times one digit in 14 runs, the second row in 355 runs, 200 of 200.
That result was known when these predictions were written. The other five arm-seeds weren't.

## The question

From a third exchange with the owner. Low-level search is what's been failing. The schema should emerge from spatial
experience: over many experiences, the overlaps are distilled into hubs that connect them, and search becomes completion.
In driving terms, the bike, the go-kart and walking uphill overlap in "more load, more push". When the car shudders,
that hub completes and the push lands on the gas. The owner first thought of a second volume. Then he asked whether the
super-assemblies already formed could be built on, with hubs over hubs, and the assistant agreed to try that small step
first. It also pointed out that hubs made of shared positions can't capture a relation like "more load, more push", so
two versions are compared.

## The setup

C185 (anchored at the best so far, widening breadth-first from the trouble, inclusion not totals; the map, residue with a
sparse sample, convergence), with hubs added:

- **Forming hubs.** When adapting starts, every pair of kept ways of working that hasn't been compared yet is compared,
  and what they share becomes a hub: an assembly joined to the shared things and to both experiences (inert marks in the
  substrate). Hubs are compared with hubs in turn, and anything new they share forms a hub of hubs, for up to 6 rounds.
  There's no sparse sampling of the overlap in this taste (declared). Comparing everything at that one moment stands in
  for the owner's continuous background comparing.
- **Two arms:**
  - **Hubs by position:** what's shared is pieces by position, the same part at the same place. Used to hold the shared
    core: parts in a hub give way last.
  - **Hubs by relation:** what's shared is relations, an offset between two parts (how one sits relative to another).
    Used for completion: after one part moves, each part related to it snaps to where the most widely shared relation
    between them puts it, if it can act there. The result is tried as one change, before the plain single change.
- **How widely shared** a relation is: the number of distinct experiences connected through the hubs holding it.
- The same three seeds as C183 to C185, each run alone.

## Predictions

1. **Times one digit:** easy in every arm and seed, within 20 runs.
2. **Hubs by position: about the same as C185** (1 of 3 seeds). Holding the shared core saves some tries, but positions
   can't carry a relation, so it shouldn't help with the pair.
3. **Hubs by relation: better than C185,** learning the second row in at least 2 of 3 seeds (seed 1 already seen to
   succeed). Completion should carry related parts along: the carry write with the carry read, and parts kept in their
   relation to the hold and the sign.
4. **Risks:**
   - **A relation shared between earlier lessons may be wrong for the second row.** Where the answer sits relative to
     the carry differs between rows, so completion can pull a part to the wrong place. It's tried as a separate change,
     so the plain change is still available.
   - **Many hubs from the six equal adding ways of working** may flood the support counts.
