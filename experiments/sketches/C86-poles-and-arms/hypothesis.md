# C86 · Poles and arms: hypothesis

Written 15 November 2026, from the owner's picture in [flow and heat](../../../docs/theories/flow-and-heat.md): settled
structure as poles (slow memory: "cat", "x", "5", "ABCD"), and fast, temporary bonds as arms that turn on them ("x" is
"5" for now). C82 and C83 found that in one volume a quick bond and a lasting one look the same: the loosening that
rebinds "x" cleanly also forgets "ABCD" when a rival arrives.

**The change:** two volumes of the same machinery (model.md). The slow one is the core. The fast one is C82's copy,
with loosening, and it is fed by the slow one's winners, so its assemblies are over slow cells and relearn nothing.

**Hypothesis:** with the two kinds of bond in different volumes, "x" can be rebound cleanly (in the fast volume) while
"ABCD" survives its rivals (in the slow one). One volume can't do both: the core keeps "ABCD" but not "x", and
loosening the reverse.

**A risk seen while building:** which volume answers a question is not something the machine decides. Reading "x" from
the fast volume and "ABC" from the slow one is the experimenter choosing. So every probe is read from both volumes, and
from the two together, as one machine would have to answer.
