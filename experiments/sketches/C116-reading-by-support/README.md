# C116 · Reading by support

C114 and C115's learning (differentiation), read three ways: cheapest (as before), AMP's competition with support as
ways in (the number of comparisons that formed a piece), and cheapest with naming by ways in. AMP's competition fixes
the slips (`and most [things]` instead of `[tthing]`), reads 58 to 83% of the text instead of 41 to 63%, and reads two
to three times as many words whole. Its cuts overall are well clear of chance for the first time. But short pieces
have the most ways in, so fragments like `im`, `en`, `ed`, `re` get in, and the clean share falls to about half.
Naming by ways in changes almost nothing. Open: how support should combine "how much of it the input backs" with "how
many ways in".
