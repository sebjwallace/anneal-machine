Layers of snapshots: C15, from the specific surface to the general depths

Each layer is made of snapshot columns (C14), with a falling threshold: the surface makes a new column unless something is 90% similar, so it keeps nearly everything in high fidelity; each layer below needs less similarity, so it has fewer, more general columns. By default the surface stays as captured and deeper columns slowly average what they win. Cells link each flash's winners across all layers. The picture comes from the most specific layer that recognised the flash (marked ANSWERING); if none did, from the deepest. Try a held-out pattern, or turn pattern noise up, to see deeper layers answer. LF: every layer sees the flash; LA: each layer sees the winning picture from the layer above.
